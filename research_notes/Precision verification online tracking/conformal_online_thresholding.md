# Online/Adaptive Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Threshold Calibration for Streaming Re-ID/Verification Scores

## What is online/adaptive conformal prediction (ACI and 2022–2025 follow-ups), and how would it apply to calibrating an accept/reject threshold on a non-stationary, class-imbalanced streaming similarity score?

### Takeaway
Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI, Gibbs & Candès 2021) and its successors reframe threshold-setting as an online gradient-descent problem on a target miscoverage/error rate: after each frame's outcome is revealed, the threshold (equivalently, a significance/quantile parameter) is nudged up or down by a step proportional to whether the last decision was "wrong," giving a provable long-run error-rate guarantee without any distributional assumptions on the score stream, including under arbitrary (even adversarial) drift. The 2024 JMLR follow-up removes ACI's main weakness — the need to hand-tune the step size to the (unknown) rate of drift — by adapting the step size online, and other 2023 work (SAOCP, PID control) adds guarantees over *every* sub-interval of time (not just the whole stream), which is the more relevant guarantee for a drifting video where "long-run average" coverage can mask long localized failure stretches.

### Cited Findings
- ACI's core update is: maintain a parameter α_t (effectively controlling the quantile/threshold used at time t); after observing whether the realized outcome at time t was an "error" (err_t ∈ {0,1} relative to nominal miscoverage rate α), update α_{t+1} = α_t + γ(α − err_t); this requires only O(1) memory/compute per step and needs only the ML model's point/quantile output, not distributional assumptions — it "provably achieves the desired coverage frequency over long-time intervals irrespective of the true data generating process," dropping the exchangeability assumption of classical conformal prediction — [Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift, Gibbs & Candès, NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/0d441de75945e5acbc865406fc9a2559-Paper.pdf).
- The 2024 JMLR follow-up ("Conformal Inference for Online Prediction with Arbitrary Distribution Shifts") states explicitly that the original ACI "suffer[s] from over-weighting historical data and thus may fail to quickly react to the underlying dynamics," and fixes this by adding a step where ACI's gradient-descent step-size γ is itself tuned over time; the new procedure has "provably small regret over all local time intervals of a given width," and — critically for a non-stationary video stream — "unlike ACI, which requires knowledge of the rate of change of the data-generating mechanism, [it] is adaptive to both the size and type of the distribution shift," and works with any baseline point- or quantile-predictor, without distributional assumptions — [Gibbs & Candès, JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/22-1218/22-1218.pdf) (abstract via [JMLR page](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v25/22-1218.html)).
- "Improved Online Conformal Prediction via Strongly Adaptive Online Learning" (Bhatnagar et al., ICML 2023) introduces SAOCP, which targets *strongly adaptive regret* — worst-case regret over **all** intervals of a fixed length simultaneously, not just the full horizon — via a meta-algorithm that runs multiple "expert" quantile-trackers each active only on its own finite-lifetime sub-interval, giving near-optimal guarantees at every timescale and "approximately valid coverage" even under changing environments — [Bhatnagar et al., ICML 2023 / arXiv:2302.07869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.07869), also [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qqMcym6AmS).
- "Conformal PID Control for Time Series Prediction" (Angelopoulos, Candès, Tibshirani, NeurIPS 2023) reframes the online threshold-update as a PID (proportional-integral-derivative) controller: the "integral" term is essentially an ACI-style online-FDR-inspired quantile tracker that accumulates the target-vs-observed error signal over time to correct systematic bias/drift, while an added "derivative"/"scorecaster" term forecasts short-term deviations (seasonality/trend) to further reduce reactive lag; the framework "adapt[s] to the presence of systematic errors due to seasonality, trends, and general distribution shifts," validated on COVID-19 death forecasting (improving on the CDC ensemble), electricity demand, market returns, and temperature — [Angelopoulos, Candès, Tibshirani, NeurIPS 2023 / arXiv:2307.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16895).
- Conformal prediction methods for object detection (a task structurally close to a similarity/confidence-threshold verification problem) have been built on top of detectors such as Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, YOLO11 and RT-DETRv2 by "refactoring model outputs into normalized confidence distributions" and applying non-conformity scores (Top-K, APS, Hinge, Margin, Brier) to get prediction sets with a formal 1−α containment guarantee "for any data distribution, sample size, and predictive model" — [Inductive Conformal Prediction for Guaranteed Class-Label Coverage in Object Detection, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13514218/); railway-signal and runway-detection variants extend this with conformal risk control for safety-critical accept/reject-style decisions — [Confident Object Detection via Conformal Prediction and Conformal Risk Control, arXiv:2304.06052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.06052); [Robust Vision-Based Runway Detection through Conformal Prediction and Conformal mAP, arXiv:2505.16740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16740).

### Inferences
- For a streaming cosine-similarity/detection-confidence accept-reject decision, ACI/PID-style methods map directly: treat "was the accept/reject decision correct" (when ground truth or a trusted proxy is available) as the err_t signal, and let the threshold move via a single scalar update per frame — this is far cheaper than re-running full conformal calibration and requires no batch calibration set collected in advance, satisfying the "usable causally" and "lightweight" constraints in the objective.
- The 2024 JMLR step-size-free variant and SAOCP's multi-timescale guarantee are the more appropriate building blocks than vanilla 2021 ACI for a video stream, precisely because video drift rate is unknown and non-uniform (scene cuts, lighting changes, subject-specific drift) — vanilla ACI's fixed γ would need to be tuned per-video/per-scenario, defeating the "not hand-tuned" goal in the assignment.
- None of these methods natively handle severe class imbalance (rare positive/target-present frames) — they control an aggregate long-run error rate over *all* frames processed, which under strong imbalance can be satisfied almost entirely by getting the majority (negative) class right while providing weak control specifically on the rare positive class; class-conditional/Mondrian variants (see class-imbalance section below) address this but are not yet fused with the online/ACI mechanics in a single off-the-shelf streaming algorithm in the literature found here.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a closed-form regret/coverage bound specialized to the binary "accept/reject on similarity score" setting with a rare positive class; all bounds found are for generic online quantile/coverage regret and would need adaptation (e.g., running class-conditional ACI streams) to give guarantees separately for the rare "match" class.
- Exact algebraic form of the PID controller's P/I/D terms (beyond the high-level description above) could not be extracted from the fetched PDFs (binary/encoded content defeated text extraction); the qualitative description above is corroborated by multiple independent secondary summaries but the precise update equations should be verified directly against the paper before implementation.

## Are there conformal/distribution-free calibration techniques specifically for open-set recognition, anomaly detection, or re-identification/verification in a streaming/online setting?

### Takeaway
Yes for anomaly detection (there is a direct lineage from "conformal anomaly detection" through modern adaptive/streaming variants) and yes, very recently, for open-set classification and visual place recognition/verification — but true *streaming, ground-truth-free, online-updating* conformal verification for re-ID specifically is thin; most re-ID-adjacent conformal work (open-set classification, visual place recognition) is still split-conformal (offline calibration set) rather than online-adaptive, and explicit application to person/object re-identification under drift is essentially unaddressed in the literature found.

### Cited Findings
- Conformal anomaly detection has an early streaming precedent: "Conformal prediction for distribution-independent anomaly detection in streaming vessel data" applies conformal prediction as "a novel approach for distribution-independent on-line learning and anomaly detection," where an observation is flagged anomalous if it falls outside the conformal prediction set at a chosen significance level — [ACM DL, 2010](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1833280.1833287).
- "Conformal k-NN Anomaly Detector for Univariate Data Streams" gives a model-free method that "adapts to non-stationarity in the data stream and provides probabilistic abnormality scores based on the conformal prediction paradigm" — [Ishimtsev et al., PMLR v60](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v60/ishimtsev17a/ishimtsev17a.pdf).
- A 2025/2026-era paper, "Adaptive Conformal Anomaly Detection with Time Series Foundation Models for Signal Monitoring," combines a foundation-model backbone with adaptive conformal scoring to "yield interpretable and calibrated thresholds for reliable streaming anomaly detection" — [arXiv:2604.20122](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20122) / [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=7uFbs68MSI).
- For open-set classification specifically, "Conformal Inference for Open-Set and Imbalanced Classification" (2025) extends conformal p-values to test whether a new point belongs to a previously-unseen class, using a connection to species-sampling models from classical statistics, and "delivers valid coverage in challenging open-set regimes where existing methods fail" while adjusting per-class thresholds to counter severe imbalance — [arXiv:2510.13037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13037). Based on the fetched content, this method is **split-conformal / batch**: it requires a held-out calibration set to compute non-conformity quantiles, with no discussed streaming/online-update variant.
- For a verification-style task structurally very close to re-ID, "SAFEVPR: Patch-Based Conformal Verification for Safe Cross-Condition Sequence Visual Place Recognition" builds a patch-level conformal predictor over DINOv2 features to produce a candidate-location set with a user-specified coverage guarantee (~90%), and explicitly leverages **temporal/sequence consistency across consecutive frames to reject false positives while preserving the statistical guarantee** — the method is described as processing sequential query images frame-by-frame, making it applicable to a real-time streaming verification pipeline — [arXiv:2605.28048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28048).
- "Conformal Privacy Auditing: Calibrated Re-identification Attacks with Statistical Guarantees" formulates re-identification auditing as constructing conformal ambiguity sets as privacy certificates — this is conceptually adjacent (conformal + re-identification) but addresses privacy-attack auditing, not verification/accept-reject calibration of a similarity score — [arXiv:2609.21340](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21340).

### Inferences
- SAFEVPR is the closest analogue found to the user's exact problem (streaming similarity-based verification with sequential/temporal structure, cross-condition drift, formal coverage guarantee) and its "temporal consistency to reject false positives while maintaining the statistical guarantee" design pattern is directly transferable to a re-ID accept/reject pipeline: instead of (or in addition to) an ACI-style single-scalar threshold update, one can conformalize per-frame candidate sets and use track-level temporal aggregation to sharpen the decision while preserving guarantees.
- The near-total absence of literature combining (a) online/adaptive conformal updating (ACI/PID/SAOCP) with (b) open-set/verification-style non-conformity scores and (c) severe class imbalance in one system suggests this specific combination is a genuine research gap — practitioners currently must compose pieces from separate literatures (online conformal mechanics + class-conditional/Mondrian calibration + open-set non-conformity scoring) rather than use a single validated off-the-shelf method.

### Gaps
- No source found applies ACI/PID/SAOCP-style *online threshold updating* to a re-identification or open-set verification score specifically; the open-set conformal work found (2510.13037) is batch/split-conformal, and the streaming verification work found (SAFEVPR) does not clearly state whether its conformal calibration itself adapts online across the video or is fixed from an initial calibration set. This distinction (fixed-but-guaranteed vs. continuously-adapting-online) could not be resolved from the fetched abstract/summary content and should be checked against the full SAFEVPR paper before relying on it for a "no future data, continuously adapting" use case.
- No literature found specifically on conformal calibration for face/object re-ID embedding cosine-similarity thresholds (as opposed to detection confidence or classification scores); this is likely because re-ID/verification is usually framed as open-set classification or metric learning rather than conformal literature's typical classification/regression framing.

## What are practical, lightweight alternatives to full conformal prediction for online threshold calibration under drift (P², CUSUM/Page-Hinkley, EWMA quantile tracking), and how do they compare to conformal methods?

### Takeaway
Streaming quantile trackers (P² algorithm; generalized EWMA quantile tracking) and change-detection-triggered threshold resets (Page-Hinkley/CUSUM) are cheap, well-understood, and easy to implement in O(1) memory/compute, but they generally lack the finite-sample, distribution-free *coverage/error-rate guarantee* that ACI-style online conformal methods provide; in fact, the core update rule of modern EWMA quantile trackers is mathematically the same stochastic-approximation-on-pinball-loss idea as ACI's gradient step, so the "lightweight alternative" and "conformal" categories are converging rather than being fundamentally different — the meaningful difference is that ACI explicitly ties the step to a user-specified target error rate and proves regret bounds, while classical streaming-quantile/CUSUM methods are typically justified only by asymptotic consistency or empirical performance.

### Cited Findings
- The P² ("Piecewise-Parabolic") algorithm computes an online quantile estimate without storing observations, using O(1) fixed memory regardless of stream length; it gives only an approximate quantile (no formal finite-sample error bound), "usually sufficient for typical telemetry use cases" — [Jain & Chlamtac, CACM 1985, via ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/4372.4378); a controlled extension ("Online Percentile Estimation," OPE) adds a control loop with alarm/threshold mechanisms for testing observed values against a statistical counter model — [Journal of Signal Processing Systems](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11265-021-01673-z).
- A "generalized exponentially weighted average" (EWA/EWMA) quantile tracker adjusts its step size online, proportional to the gap between the current observation and the current quantile estimate — "if the estimator is off-track ... large steps will be taken to promptly get the estimator back on-track" — and its convergence to the true quantile is proven via stochastic-approximation theory (not a finite-sample coverage bound); it is shown empirically to outperform legacy trackers (including P²) in adaptivity under non-stationary streams — [Hammer, Yazidi & Rue, "Quantile Tracking in Dynamically Varying Data Streams," arXiv:1901.04681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.04681) and [Applied Intelligence 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10489-018-1335-7).
- The Page-Hinkley (PH) test is a CUSUM-type sequential change-detection statistic: it accumulates the deviation of each new observation from a running mean (adjusted by a tolerance parameter) and signals drift once the cumulative statistic exceeds a threshold; it is "well-suited for online settings ... processes one data point at a time without needing to store the entire history" and is widely used for concept-drift detection to trigger model/threshold retraining — [GeeksforGeeks summary](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/artificial-intelligence/page-hinkley-method/); [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/abdulrehman2050/detecting-change-in-time-series-data-2fp8).
- Adaptive-threshold variants of CUSUM exist specifically to fix a constant false-alarm rate over time (rather than a fixed statistic threshold): "an adaptive threshold estimation scheme ... based on a criterion that ensures a constant rate of false alarms throughout the process ... adapt[ing] to the time evolution of the probability distribution of the test statistic" — [Adaptive threshold computation for CUSUM-type procedures, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947308000212). This is conceptually the closest classical-statistics analogue to ACI's target-error-rate tracking, but is a hypothesis-testing/false-alarm-rate framework rather than a distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantee.

### Inferences
- Implementation-complexity-vs-guarantee-strength ordering, from the sources gathered: (1) fixed/static threshold — trivial, no guarantee, breaks immediately under drift; (2) P²/EWMA streaming quantile tracker — trivial (single running estimate, O(1) state), asymptotic consistency only, no explicit target-error-rate framing; (3) Page-Hinkley/CUSUM change detection + threshold reset — slightly more state (cumulative statistic, tolerance/threshold hyperparameters), gives an alarm-rate property under distributional assumptions on the test statistic, but conflates "detecting a shift occurred" with "having the right threshold right now"; (4) adaptive-threshold CUSUM (constant false-alarm-rate variant) — closer to a formal guarantee but requires modeling the test statistic's distribution; (5) ACI/PID/SAOCP online conformal — same O(1) per-step cost as EWMA quantile tracking, but explicitly targets a user-specified error rate and proves regret/coverage bounds that hold for *arbitrary* (not just parametrically-modeled) drift, making it strictly preferable when the implementation cost is comparable.
- Because the EWMA quantile tracker's update rule and ACI's update rule are structurally the same stochastic-approximation step, an ACI-style implementation is not meaningfully more complex than a "simple" EWMA/P² quantile tracker — the extra value from ACI/PID is the formal regret guarantee and (in the 2024 JMLR variant) automatic step-size tuning, essentially "for free" relative to a hand-tuned EWMA decay rate. This argues for preferring ACI-family methods over ad hoc EWMA/P² thresholds whenever a guarantee is wanted, and reserving CUSUM/Page-Hinkley for a complementary role: detecting abrupt regime changes (e.g., scene cuts, camera handoff) that might warrant resetting or re-warming the online conformal tracker's state, since ACI-style methods handle *gradual* drift well via their gradient step but can react slowly to a sudden step-change without an explicit reset/restart signal.

### Gaps
- No source directly benchmarks ACI/PID conformal methods against P²/EWMA/CUSUM on a shared streaming re-ID or verification task; the comparison above is inferred from separately-described properties of each method family rather than a head-to-head empirical study, and should be flagged as an inference, not a cited finding.
- No source quantifies how much implementation/compute overhead PID's "derivative"/scorecaster term adds relative to a plain ACI update or a plain EWMA quantile tracker.

## Can online conformal give an explicit, tunable precision/recall or false-positive-rate operating point that self-adjusts online (not just "a" threshold)?

### Takeaway
Yes — this is essentially the native design of ACI-family methods (you supply a target miscoverage/error rate α and the threshold self-adjusts to try to track it over time), and it generalizes formally to arbitrary monotone risk functions (including false-positive/false-negative rate) via Conformal Risk Control; a closely related and independently-developed toolset for the same goal is online false discovery rate (FDR) control (LORD, SAFFRON), which explicitly maintains a target false-positive budget across a sequential stream of accept/reject decisions.

### Cited Findings
- ACI's defining feature is that the practitioner sets a target error rate α up front, and the online update α_t → α_t + γ(α − err_t) is explicitly a control loop trying to drive the realized error rate to the chosen α over time, regardless of the underlying data-generating process — [Gibbs & Candès 2021](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/0d441de75945e5acbc865406fc9a2559-Paper.pdf).
- "Conformal Risk Control" (Angelopoulos, Bates, et al.) generalizes split conformal prediction to "control the expected value of any monotone loss function," with the guarantee "tight up to an O(1/n) factor," and has been used to bound quantities like false negative rate — the framework is explicitly about choosing a parameter so that "an expected loss bound holds," i.e., letting a user specify a target risk level (e.g., a maximum acceptable false-negative or false-positive rate) and having the calibration find/adjust the corresponding threshold — [arXiv:2208.02814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.02814), published at ICLR 2024 ([proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/f3549ef9b5ff520a7e41ff3cc306ab2b-Paper-Conference.pdf)); extensions exist for non-monotonic losses — [Conformal Risk Control under Non-Monotone Losses, arXiv:2604.01502](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01502).
- Online false discovery rate (FDR) control methods — LORD (LORD++) and SAFFRON — are a separately-developed but conceptually parallel line of work: "online testing procedures assume that hypotheses are observed in sequence, and allow the significance thresholds for upcoming tests to depend on the test statistics observed so far," explicitly maintaining a false-discovery budget (an FDR target) across a live sequential decision stream, with SAFFRON described as incorporating "adaptive estimates of the proportion of true null hypotheses" and representing "the current state-of-the-art in online FDR control" — [Ramdas et al., SAFFRON, PMLR v80](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ramdas18a/ramdas18a.pdf); robustness of LORD/SAFFRON's FDR guarantee under positive dependence between sequential test statistics (relevant since consecutive video frames are highly dependent, not independent) is established in [Fisher, Biometrical Journal 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bimj.202300177).
- Angelopoulos/Candès/Tibshirani's Conformal PID Control paper frames the integral term explicitly as tracking a target coverage/error level over time and correcting systematic deviations from it due to trend/seasonality, i.e., the same "user specifies α, system self-adjusts threshold to hit α" pattern as ACI, but with added correction for structured (not just stochastic) drift — [arXiv:2307.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16895).

### Inferences
- For the user's stated goal ("I want at most X% false positive rate and have the threshold self-adjust to try to maintain that online"), the most directly matching primitive is an ACI-style update where err_t is defined specifically as a false-accept event (rather than generic miscoverage): define α = target FPR, and update the threshold using only the stream of accept-decisions-on-known/inferred-negatives, decoupling the FPR control loop from the FNR/recall side — this requires being able to identify, even approximately, which decisions are on negatives, which is a nontrivial requirement (see next section on noisy feedback).
- Online FDR control (LORD/SAFFRON) is arguably a *more* natural fit than ACI for a "target X% false-positive rate among accept decisions" framing, since FDR is defined exactly as (false accepts)/(total accepts) — closer to precision-of-the-accepted-set than to a coverage rate over all frames — while ACI/conformal risk control's α is more naturally a per-frame error/miscoverage rate. Both are online, sequential, and give formal non-asymptotic guarantees; which is more appropriate depends on whether the user's "X% false positive rate" means "FPR relative to all negative frames" (ACI/conformal-risk-control framing) or "fraction of accepted frames that are actually wrong / false-discovery-proportion" (online-FDR framing, closer to precision).
- To get an independent, simultaneous recall (or FNR) target in addition to an FPR target — i.e., a genuine two-sided precision/recall operating point rather than a single error rate — the literature suggests running two coupled online-conformal/risk-control loops (one per side) or using conformal risk control with a loss function that jointly penalizes both, but no source found gives a ready-made "two-threshold" or PR-curve-level online conformal recipe; this would need to be assembled from the monotone-risk-control framework rather than taken off the shelf.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a worked example or algorithm that simultaneously targets both a precision (or FPR) constraint and a recall (or FNR) constraint online with formal guarantees on both — the current literature (ACI, conformal risk control, online FDR) targets one scalar risk/error rate at a time.
- No source found discusses how online FDR control (LORD/SAFFRON) behaves under the strong serial dependence of consecutive video frames beyond the general "positive dependence" robustness result cited above; video frame scores are typically far more dependent (smoothly correlated) than the "local dependence" structures analyzed in that literature, so the guarantee's applicability to a real video stream is plausible but not directly verified by a cited source.

## What are the known failure modes of online conformal methods under severe class imbalance and noisy/self-labeled feedback (no ground truth, only the model's own past decisions)?

### Takeaway
Two independent, serious failure modes are documented: (1) marginal (non-class-conditional) conformal guarantees can be satisfied almost entirely by the majority class while giving near-useless coverage on the rare positive class — with one cited example dropping to ~53% coverage on the minority/anomaly class at a 1:345 imbalance ratio — and (2) online conformal's error-rate guarantee provably degrades (from O(1/T) to a term that scales linearly with the corruption/noise rate, or worse under adversarial/directionally-biased corruption) when the feedback signal used to update the threshold is itself wrong, which is exactly the "no ground truth, only self-generated accept/reject decisions" scenario in the key question.

### Cited Findings
- Under class imbalance, "class imbalance reduces the precision of the conformal predictor due to a sparsity in calibration examples belonging to the rare class," and "the marginal coverage guarantee ensures that the true label is covered across the entire test distribution, but this does not guarantee adequate coverage for minority classes" — in one cited extreme case, "standard conformal prediction may achieve only 52.94% anomaly coverage at a 1:345 imbalance ratio," while class-conditional (Mondrian) conformal calibration — computing quantiles separately per class — "maintain[ed] 90.59% anomaly coverage" — [search-result synthesis citing Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction literature; MDPI paper "Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction for Reliable Anomaly Detection Under Extreme Class Imbalance" (full text access blocked, HTTP 403) referenced via search summary](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/8/7/190); related class-conditional framing also appears in [Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction for Imbalanced Data via Top-k Classes, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Dtxc7mlKRg&noteId=O89GG0rCn2) and [Reliable Uncertainty Under Class Imbalance and Distribution Shift, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13193150/).
- "Online Conformal Prediction with Corrupted Feedback" directly analyzes the exact scenario in the key question — feedback used to update the online threshold is corrupted (modeled as the learner receiving a corrupted miscoverage indicator ē_t instead of the true e_t). Its Theorem 1 shows standard online conformal prediction's miscoverage bound, normally O(1/T), degrades to include additional terms that scale with the *directional imbalance* of the corruption (i.e., whether corruption tends to bias errors one way, e.g., systematically making rejects look like accepts) — under i.i.d. stochastic corruption with rate p, standard online conformal prediction's guarantee becomes MisCov(T) ≤ O(1/T) + 2p, i.e., a **persistent, non-vanishing bias proportional to the corruption rate**, rather than converging to the target — [arXiv:2605.20515](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20515v1).
- The same paper proposes two mitigations — a filtering variant (F-ROCP) that exploits the fact that when the threshold parameter exits its valid range, the true gradient direction is known regardless of corrupted feedback, and an active-compensation variant (AC-ROCP) that periodically forces the threshold to boundary values to infer/estimate the corruption pattern and compensate for it, achieving a vanishing-error guarantee (O(√|I|)/T) even under bounded-memory adversarial corruption, as opposed to standard online conformal's persistent bias — [arXiv:2605.20515](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20515v1).
- Separately, conformal prediction has documented (non-online) robustness to *pure random label noise*: "naive conformal prediction covers the noiseless ground truth label unless the noise distribution is adversarially designed," per label-noise-robustness analyses — [Label Noise Robustness of Conformal Prediction, JMLR 2024](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1549/23-1549.pdf) / [arXiv:2209.14295](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14295) — but this robustness result is for i.i.d.-style random noise in an offline/exchangeable setting, not for the systematic, self-referential bias that arises when the "labels" are the model's own thresholded decisions (which are correlated with the current threshold itself, unlike independent random noise).
- "Conformal-in-the-Loop" (CitL) explicitly targets the combined imbalance+noise setting (as opposed to treating them separately, which the paper states is standard practice and yields suboptimal results); it uses conformal-prediction-derived sample uncertainty to reweight/prune unreliable examples during training, reporting up to 6.1% accuracy and 5.0 mIoU improvements — though this is a *training-time* data-cleaning use of conformal prediction rather than an online streaming-threshold-calibration method, so its lessons transfer only partially — [arXiv:2411.02281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02281) (abstract fetched; full-text extraction of methodology failed due to binary PDF encoding).

### Inferences
- The "corrupted feedback" failure mode is the single most load-bearing finding for this project's actual deployment condition: because there is no ground truth at inference time, the online conformal threshold would necessarily be updated using the system's *own* accept/reject decisions as a proxy for correctness (e.g., "assume the decision was right unless some downstream signal disagrees"), which is structurally exactly the corrupted/noisy-feedback setting analyzed in arXiv:2605.20515 — and that paper shows this produces a **persistent bias in the operating point**, not just added variance, under standard ACI-style updating. This means a naive self-labeled online conformal loop will likely converge to a *systematically wrong* threshold (e.g., chronically too permissive or too strict) rather than merely a noisier one, unless a filtering/compensation mechanism (as in F-ROCP/AC-ROCP) or an independent (even if infrequent/weak) source of ground-truth-adjacent feedback is incorporated.
- Combining the class-imbalance finding and the corrupted-feedback finding compounds the risk: with the positive/target-present class already rare (10–20% of frames per the assignment's framing), and self-labeled feedback being least reliable exactly on the hardest/most ambiguous frames (which disproportionately occur near the true decision boundary, i.e., near where the rare positive class lives), the two failure modes likely interact — errors on the rare class are both statistically under-weighted (imbalance) and specifically the ones most likely to be mislabeled by the model's own self-assessment (corrupted feedback concentrated where the model is least confident), suggesting naive marginal online conformal calibration is a poor fit for this deployment without both a class-conditional (or track/positive-conditional) calibration split and an explicit corruption-aware update rule.

### Gaps
