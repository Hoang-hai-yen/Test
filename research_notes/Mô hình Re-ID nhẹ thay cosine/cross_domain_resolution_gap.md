# Closing the Domain Gap Between Clean Reference Images and Degraded Video Crops (Cross-Resolution / Cross-Quality Instance Matching)

## What is "cross-resolution re-identification" and what are the established techniques for asymmetric-quality matching?

### Takeaway
Cross-Resolution Re-ID (CR-ReID, also "low-resolution re-ID") is a well-established sub-field (dating to ~2018-2019, still active through 2025-2026) that specifically studies matching a high-resolution gallery/reference against a low-resolution probe/query. Four main technique families exist — super-resolution preprocessing, resolution-invariant embedding learning, adversarial domain adaptation, and (more recently) lightweight post-hoc feature-alignment modules — and the field has explicitly moved away from SR-based preprocessing toward feature-space solutions because SR cannot recover information that was never captured.

### Cited Findings
- CR-ReID is formally defined as the task of matching images of the same identity captured at different resolutions in different scenarios/cameras — [Dual-Resolution Fusion Modeling for Unsupervised Cross-Resolution Person Re-Identification, ACM MM 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3664647.3681067)
- Most conventional CR-ReID works use Super-Resolution (SR) models to upscale low-resolution images to high-resolution before matching, but "SR models cannot completely compensate for the missing information in LR images," leaving a persistent gap between SR-restored images and real HR images — [Multi-Scale Image- and Feature-Level Alignment for Cross-Resolution Person Re-Identification, Remote Sensing 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16020278)
- Resolution-invariant representation learning (e.g., Foreground-Focus Super-Resolution + Resolution-Invariant Feature Extractor, "FFSR+RIFE") jointly trains an upscaling module and a dual-attention two-stream feature extractor end-to-end so that the final embedding is resolution-agnostic — [Resolution-invariant Person Re-Identification, IJCAI 2019 / arXiv:1906.09748](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09748)
- RAIN (Resolution Adaptation and re-Identification Network) uses adversarial learning — a cross-resolution feature extractor, an HR decoder, and a discriminator that aligns feature distributions across resolutions — to force the backbone to learn resolution-invariant features end-to-end, and can generalize to resolutions not seen during training — [Learning Resolution-Invariant Deep Representations for Person Re-Identification, AAAI 2019 / arXiv:1907.10843](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.10843)
- A newer line of work observes that resolution-induced feature discrepancy follows "a consistent, resolution-related semantic direction in the embedding space," and proposes Vector-Panning Feature Alignment (VPFA) — a lightweight 3-layer MLP with residual connections and a Tanh gate — applied purely post-hoc on top of a frozen backbone's features, requiring no backbone retraining. It outperformed SR-based PS-HRNet and INTACT on MLR-Market-1501 (94.1% vs 91.5% Rank-1) and MLR-CUHK03 (94.7% vs 92.6%), and beat the prior best on the real-world CAVIAR dataset (66.6% vs 63.6% Rank-1) — [Resolution as a Direction: Vector-Panning Feature Alignment for Cross-Resolution Re-Identification, arXiv:2510.00936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00936)
- Knowledge-distillation approaches for cross-resolution matching exist under names like "Resolution based Feature Distillation," where a model trained on HR images acts as a teacher and transfers structural/relational knowledge to a model handling LR inputs — [Resolution based Feature Distillation for Cross Resolution Person Re-Identification, arXiv:2109.07871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07871)
- Unsupervised cross-resolution methods (no labeled target-resolution data) use dual-resolution fusion combined with contrastive learning to bridge HR/LR domains without full supervision — [Dual-Resolution Fusion Modeling for Unsupervised Cross-Resolution Person Re-Identification, ACM MM 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3664647.3681067)
- The standard training protocol for CR-ReID benchmarks (MLR-Market-1501, MLR-CUHK03, MLR-VIPeR) synthesizes the "low-resolution" side of training pairs by **downsampling** the original high-resolution training images by a random factor — i.e., the field's default data-generation recipe already is "degrade the clean image to create the matching low-quality counterpart" — [Resolution as a Direction: Vector-Panning Feature Alignment, arXiv:2510.00936](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00936) (MLR protocol description); consistent with earlier framing in [Multi-Scale Image- and Feature-Level Alignment, Remote Sensing 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16020278)
- A 2025 survey/technical work notes cross-camera ReID in smart cities is "severely hindered" by low-resolution images causing detail loss and weakened identity features, and lists hybrid attention transformer (HAT) SR, pixel/semantic-adjustable SR, and lightweight omni-aggregation SR networks as the current SR-based mitigation tools — [Person Re-Identification Enhanced by Super-Resolution Technology, Electronics 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics14234647)

### Inferences
- The field has been trending away from pure SR-preprocessing (2019-era) toward (a) resolution-invariant joint training with adversarial or distillation losses (needs paired/labeled target-domain data) and (b) very lightweight, post-hoc, frozen-backbone feature-alignment modules like VPFA (2025) that need far less data and no backbone retraining — this second category is the most promising direction for a low-data, minimal-reference use case.
- Because SR cannot invent missing detail, the community consensus implicit in these papers is that trying to "clean up" a degraded query is fundamentally capped — which supports investigating the reverse direction (degrading the clean reference) as a valid alternative research direction rather than a workaround.

### Gaps
- None of the surveyed CR-ReID papers benchmark the specific "3-reference-images-per-instance, generic-object" setting; all use large labeled person-ReID datasets (Market-1501, CUHK03, VIPeR) with thousands of identities for training the resolution-invariant/adversarial/distillation components.

## Are there specific techniques for aerial/drone-view instance matching that address the altitude/viewpoint/compression gap?

### Takeaway
Aerial-ground (sky-to-ground) re-ID is now an active benchmark area (AG-ReID, AG-VPReID, PRAI-1581, UAV-Human), with 2025 challenge results showing that extreme altitude and viewpoint changes remain the dominant error source — worse than resolution alone — and that winning solutions rely on foundation-model backbones (CLIP/EVA-CLIP), attribute/soft-biometric filtering, and view-specific encoding layers rather than resolution-specific fixes; several findings (directional asymmetry, altitude-driven accuracy decay) generalize to any aerial-vs-ground instance-matching problem, even non-person categories.

### Cited Findings
- AG-ReID: 21,983 images of 388 identities with 15 soft attributes, collected by a UAV flying at altitudes of 15-45 m paired with a ground CCTV camera on a university campus; characterized by "extreme cross-viewpoint geometric distortions, resolution and scale variability" — [Aerial-Ground Person Re-ID, arXiv:2303.08597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08597)
- AG-VPReID (CVPR 2025): a large-scale aerial-ground **video** benchmark with 6,632 identities, 32,321 tracklets, and 9.6 million frames from drones (15-120 m altitude), CCTV, and wearable cameras — [AG-VPReID CVPR Poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/33154)
- The AG-VPReID 2025 Challenge dataset (a subset/companion release) used drones at extreme altitudes of 80-120 m, with 3,027 identities, 13,511 tracklets, ~3.7 million frames; human body sizes ranged from as small as 18×37 pixels to 293×542 pixels within the same dataset — [AG-VPReID 2025: Aerial-Ground Video-based Person Re-identification Challenge Results, arXiv:2506.22843](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22843v1)
- Methods in this challenge experienced a **4-6% Rank-1 accuracy drop** when altitude increased from 80 m to 120 m, demonstrating that altitude/viewpoint (not just resolution) is a major, separately-degrading factor — [AG-VPReID 2025 Challenge Results, arXiv:2506.22843](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22843v1)
- Ground-to-Aerial matching consistently outperformed Aerial-to-Ground matching across all challenge methods, suggesting "upward viewpoint adaptation may be more tractable than downward perspective matching" — i.e. the direction of the domain gap matters, not just its magnitude — [AG-VPReID 2025 Challenge Results, arXiv:2506.22843](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22843v1)
- The winning method (X-TFCLIP, 71.56% Rank-1) used a modified CLIP backbone (bicubic extrapolation of positional embeddings + instance normalization), attention-based frame pooling, soft-biometric (gender/age/ethnicity/body-shape) filtering of incompatible query-gallery pairs, and simple horizontal-flip augmentation with embedding averaging; the runner-up (TFCLIP-AG-VPReID, 71.89% Rank-1) instead scaled up to EVA-CLIP-L-14 and added more training data (G2AVReID) with a Video-to-Memory Contrastive Loss and hard-mining — [AG-VPReID 2025 Challenge Results, arXiv:2506.22843](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22843v1)
- A third top approach (ACP-VPReID, 67.72% Rank-1) used view-specific transformation layers per camera type (aerial/ground/wearable), temporal memory diffusion, motion-aware gating from frame differences, and scale-adaptive modules conditioned on camera type — [AG-VPReID 2025 Challenge Results, arXiv:2506.22843](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22843v1)
- PRAI-1581 is a large airborne person-ReID dataset (39,461 images, 1,581 identities) from two DJI consumer UAVs at 20-60 m altitude; person resolution ranges from only 30 to 150 pixels, and high altitude increases diversity of viewpoints/poses "more extreme" than ground-level ReID — [PRAI-1581 GitHub](https://github.com/stormyoung/PRAI-1581), [Person Re-identification in Aerial Imagery, arXiv:1908.05024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05024)
- A dedicated paper for drone-footage person-ReID under low resolution argues against heavy SR (too costly for drone/edge compute) and instead uses **attribute-guided filtering**: it found that upper/lower-body color attributes "remain robust even with severe downsampling," and uses an Attribute-Guided Metric Distillation (AMD) model to pre-filter gallery candidates by robust attributes before full ReID embedding computation, combined with fog/edge-computing to do lightweight attribute extraction at the edge rather than transmitting full video to the cloud. On Market-1501 at 16% resolution this attribute-guided approach improved Rank-1 from 88.72% to 95.31% (+6.59 pts); at 9% resolution, +5.55 pts — [Person Re-Identification with Attribute-Guided, Robust-to-Low-Resolution Drone Footage Considering Fog/Edge Computing, PMC11946598](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11946598/)
- This attribute-guided approach is comparatively lightweight on data: it reuses existing attribute annotations on Market-1501 rather than requiring new large-scale labeling, and uses synthetic data (Unreal Engine 5 + AirSim) for controlled analysis rather than large real-world collection — [PMC11946598](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11946598/)
- UAV-Human is a large multi-modal benchmark (67,428 sequences, 119 subjects) that includes a person-ReID subset (41,290 frames, 1,144 identities) captured by UAV, useful as pretraining/auxiliary data for aerial viewpoint robustness — [UAV-Human, arXiv:2104.00946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00946)
- A newer far-distance video ReID challenge (VReID-XFD, WACV 2026 workshop) specifically targets "long-range UAV-to-ground video with severe viewpoint, resolution, and appearance variation," indicating the community is converging on exactly this asymmetric-quality problem as a distinct benchmark track — [VReID-XFD Challenge Results, WACV2026W](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026W/VReID-XFD/papers/Hambarde_VReID-XFD_Video-based_Person_Re-identification_at_Extreme_Far_Distance_Challenge_Results_WACVW_2026_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The altitude-driven accuracy decay and the ground-to-aerial vs aerial-to-ground asymmetry are architecture-agnostic findings about the *viewpoint/altitude gap itself*, so they likely generalize beyond persons to any object category matched between a close-up reference and a downward aerial/drone crop — even though the underlying datasets (AG-ReID, PRAI-1581, UAV-Human) are person-specific.
- Attribute-/soft-biometric-guided filtering and CLIP/foundation-model backbones with light task-specific adaptation (flip augmentation, embedding averaging, view-specific linear layers) are the practical, relatively data-light techniques used by top aerial-ReID solutions in 2025 — heavier techniques (full backbone retraining with hard-mining contrastive loss on large aerial+ground paired datasets) gave only marginal additional gains (71.89% vs 71.56% Rank-1) despite much larger data requirements.

### Gaps
- No aerial/drone benchmark found that specifically isolates **compression** (as opposed to resolution/altitude/viewpoint) as a controlled variable; compression artifacts in aerial video streams (e.g., H.264/H.265 codec blocking) are not explicitly studied as a separate factor in any source found.
- No non-person (generic object) aerial re-identification benchmark was found; all aerial-specific literature located is person-ReID (PRAI-1581, AG-ReID, AG-VPReID, UAV-Human).

## Is degrading the clean reference images during training/embedding an effective, cheap technique vs. more complex domain adaptation — and is it benchmarked?

### Takeaway
Yes — degradation-based augmented training (synthetically corrupting training images to mimic real-world query degradations) is directly benchmarked in 2025-2026 papers and shown to be effective, cheap, and NOT dependent on large labeled datasets; it clearly outperforms naive fine-tuning and standard augmentation (AugMix), though a lightweight adapter approach (DARA) captures most of the benefit of full degraded-data fine-tuning at a small fraction of the compute/parameter cost. However, the literature located studies degrading *training* images generically (and corrupting the *query* at test time) rather than the specific asymmetric scenario the user describes (degrading only the clean gallery/reference images to match an already-degraded query domain) — that exact ablation was not found and is flagged as a gap.

### Cited Findings
- The standard "corrupted-query" (CQ) retrieval protocol in recent animal re-ID research applies synthetic corruptions **only to query images while gallery images remain unchanged**, explicitly designed to reflect "practical animal re-ID deployments where a degraded field image is used as the query and matched against original reference images in the gallery" — the same structure as the user's reference-vs-video-crop scenario — [DARA: Degradation-Aware Low-Rank Residual Adaptation, arXiv:2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16644)
- A dedicated study on **degradation-based augmented training** built three progressively more realistic degradation pipelines for training data: (1) Simple — Gaussian blur + downscaling (2x/4x) + noise; (2) Diverse — one of {Gaussian, Generalized Gaussian, Motion, Defocus} blur + one downscaling method + Gaussian noise + JPEG compression; (3) Diverse+ — all blur types randomly applied, multiple downscale factors (2-4x), noise, JPEG compression, plus post-degradation resampling; parameters: kernel sizes 3-21px, JPEG quality 30-95, noise std 0.004-0.01 — [Degradation-based augmented training for robust individual animal re-identification, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- Diverse+ degradation training achieved an **8.5% Rank-1 accuracy gain** on real-world low-clarity (clarity-4) images versus the un-augmented baseline, and a further ~7% gain from adding a learning-based (data-driven) degradation model instead of hand-crafted pipelines — [Degradation-based augmented training, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- Critically, "diverse degradation models showing increased performance over simple ones that fail to capture the complexity of real-world degradations" — meaning naive single-type degradation (e.g., just downsampling) is measurably worse than combining multiple realistic degradation types — [Degradation-based augmented training, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- This degradation-augmentation approach was explicitly confirmed to **not require large labeled datasets**: it uses only the standard training split of existing re-ID datasets (no new images), and only the optional learning-based enhancement needed modest quality-labeled data (1,514 high-clarity / 1,187 low-clarity images for one species) rather than exhaustive re-annotation — [Degradation-based augmented training, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- Generalization to individuals *not seen* during training was strong: only a 2-3% accuracy drop compared to seen individuals — relevant to the user's low-shot, per-instance setting — [Degradation-based augmented training, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- The DARA follow-up paper directly benchmarks degradation-based approaches against each other on corrupted queries (average mAP across 4 datasets: ATRW, FriesianCattle2017, MPDD, SeaStarReID2023): Standard fine-tuning 30.17% mAP → AugMix fine-tuning 41.67% mAP → DARA (lightweight adapter) 55.70% mAP → **Full Corrupted-Data Fine-tuning 63.35% mAP (best)** — i.e., directly retraining/fine-tuning on degraded data is the strongest technique, but a lightweight residual-adapter approach recovers ~77% of that gap at only 0.49% extra parameters and 0.05% extra FLOPs (0.23ms latency increase) — [DARA, arXiv:2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16644)
- DARA needs no corruption-type labels (uses ImageNet-C-style synthetic corruption operators — 8 types × 5 severities — paired against clean originals) and keeps the backbone frozen, training only small low-rank residual "expert" modules plus a lightweight router — a genuinely lightweight, minimal-data recipe (no target-category-specific large dataset, no manual corruption annotation) — [DARA, arXiv:2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16644)
- DARA also generalized across datasets (trained on FriesianCattle2017, transferred to CornwallCattle) and to unseen corruption types not seen in training — [DARA, arXiv:2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16644)
- In the broader CR-ReID literature, the standard MLR training protocol (used across MLR-Market-1501, MLR-CUHK03, MLR-VIPeR benchmarks) already builds training pairs by downsampling clean HR training images — i.e., "degrade the clean reference to synthesize the matching low-quality counterpart" is the field's default recipe for generating cross-resolution training data, not a novel workaround — [Resolution as a Direction: Vector-Panning Feature Alignment, arXiv:2510.00936](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00936)
- A separate line of general (non-reid) work on data augmentation confirms the broader principle: "training with stochastic degradation-based augmentation techniques remarkably improves performance on nearly all kinds of processed data even with intense severity, while significantly improving robustness and maintaining high performance on original unaltered data" — [Data Augmentation Can Improve Robustness, arXiv:2111.05328](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05328)

### Inferences
- For a use case with only 3 clean reference images per object instance (too little data to train a resolution-invariant embedding network, adversarial model, or distillation pipeline from scratch), the DARA-style lightweight adapter and the plain degradation-augmented-fine-tuning recipe are the most transferable techniques found: both work on top of an existing pretrained embedding backbone and use synthetic corruption of *training* data (which, generalized to the user's case, would mean synthetically degrading the reference images used to build/fine-tune the matching function) rather than requiring large labeled datasets of the target category.
- The clear ranking (Full Corrupted Fine-tuning > DARA-style lightweight adapter > AugMix generic augmentation > Standard fine-tuning) suggests that if compute/data allow full fine-tuning it's best, but if only a handful of reference images exist per instance (no room for backbone fine-tuning), the cheapest effective lever is still degrading/augmenting whatever images ARE available (the 3 references) with a realistic, diverse degradation pipeline (multiple blur types + multiple downscale factors + JPEG compression + noise) rather than a single simplistic transform.
- No source directly states that degrading the reference is *better* than degrading the query at training time — the CQ protocol (gallery clean, query corrupted) is the field's default test-time setup, but the *training-time* recipe of downsampling/degrading the clean HR side to synthesize matched pairs (from MLR-ReID protocols) is functionally equivalent to "degrade the reference" and is standard practice, not an exotic shortcut.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that explicitly ablates "degrade only the small set of clean reference/gallery images per instance, keep the matching function fixed" as a standalone technique isolated from full retraining — this exact minimal-data, index-time-only degradation strategy (as opposed to training-time degradation augmentation) does not appear to have a dedicated benchmark in the literature surveyed.
- No direct head-to-head comparison was found between "degrade the reference to look like the query domain" vs. "enhance/super-resolve the query to look like the reference domain" as two competing cheap strategies; the literature instead treats SR-based query enhancement and resolution-invariant training as largely separate research threads from degradation-augmentation training, rather than being benchmarked against each other in a single controlled study.

## What role does targeted (degradation-specific) data augmentation play in current best practice for cross-quality instance matching?

### Takeaway
Targeted, degradation-profile-matched augmentation (specific blur kernels, specific compression codecs/quality factors, specific downsampling factors) — applied primarily at **training time** — is treated in 2024-2026 literature as a first-line, cheap, largely data-agnostic technique, with evidence that realistic/diverse degradation composition beats naive single-transform augmentation, and that curriculum-style progressive introduction of harder degradations improves results further; test-time augmentation (TTA) is a secondary, lower-impact lever with documented limits when the underlying backbone or training distribution is weak.

### Cited Findings
- The wildlife re-ID degradation study found that "diverse" and "diverse+" pipelines (multiple blur types + multiple downscale factors + JPEG compression + noise, randomly combined) significantly outperformed the "simple" pipeline (single Gaussian blur + fixed downscale + noise, no compression) — confirming that matching the augmentation profile to realistic combined degradations (not just one factor like resolution) matters — [Degradation-based augmented training, arXiv:2603.04163](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04163)
- A motion-blur-specific instance retrieval paper ("Retrieval Robust to Object Motion Blur") introduces loss functions specifically designed to make representations invariant to motion blur, and constructs a synthetic training dataset plus a dedicated real-world evaluation dataset for blur-robust retrieval, indicating the retrieval community treats motion blur as its own distinct degradation axis worth dedicated augmentation/loss design rather than folding it into generic noise augmentation — [Retrieval Robust to Object Motion Blur, arXiv:2404.18025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18025)
- A blur-augmentation training strategy uses a three-stage curriculum: a "stabilization" stage with straight/random-walk blur kernels, a "mixed realism" stage adding curved/rolling-shutter blur patterns, and an "advanced" stage adding zoom-with-rotation blur — progressing from simple to complex blur types over training — demonstrating that curriculum-ordered, kernel-realistic blur augmentation improves both detection and retrieval robustness — [search synthesis based on motion blur augmentation literature, exact paper title not resolved in results — see Gaps]
- DARA's corruption pool uses standard **ImageNet-C operators** (Gaussian noise, motion blur, defocus blur, fog, brightness, contrast, JPEG compression, pixelate) at 8 types × 5 severities as the targeted degradation-profile augmentation source for training the router/adapter — [DARA, arXiv:2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16644)
- Test-time augmentation (TTA — applying augmentations at inference and aggregating predictions) is a recognized, model-agnostic, no-retraining technique, but its effectiveness is explicitly noted to be limited "if the model is trained on low-quality or imbalanced datasets" or if "the model architecture is not strong" — i.e., TTA cannot compensate for a backbone that was never exposed to the target degradation profile during training — [Understanding Test-Time Augmentation, arXiv:2402.06892](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06892v1); [search synthesis, low-quality TTA limitation]
- Selective/adaptive TTA variants (filtering which augmented views to trust, e.g., via similarity-based filtering) exist as refinements but are documented mainly in classification/multimodal retrieval settings, not explicitly instance re-ID — [Efficient improvement of classification accuracy via selective test-time augmentation, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0020025523007338)
- Beyond re-ID specifically, general robustness literature confirms stochastic degradation-based training augmentation "improves performance on nearly all kinds of processed data even with intense severity" while preserving clean-data accuracy — supporting targeted augmentation as safe to apply broadly without hurting the clean/reference-only matching case — [Data Augmentation Can Improve Robustness, arXiv:2111.05328](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05328)

### Inferences
- Current best practice (as of 2025-2026 sources) treats training-time degradation augmentation as the dominant, most cost-effective lever, with the augmentation profile ideally: (a) diverse (multiple blur kernels/types, not one), (b) matched to the real degradation pipeline (motion blur + downsampling + codec compression combined, since real video crops experience all three simultaneously), and (c) possibly curriculum-ordered (easy-to-hard). Test-time augmentation is a lightweight complementary step but is not a substitute for training-time exposure to the target degradation profile.
- For a minimal-reference-image use case, the practical recipe implied by this literature is: take the few clean reference images, apply a diverse randomized degradation pipeline (compression + blur + downsampling, similar to the wildlife re-id "diverse+" recipe) to generate synthetic degraded variants, and use these either to fine-tune/adapt a lightweight module (DARA-style) or simply to augment the reference set used for matching/indexing — since this requires no additional labeled data beyond the reference images themselves.

### Gaps
- The exact source for the "three-stage curriculum" motion-blur training strategy claim was not resolved to a single, clearly identifiable arXiv/DOI link in this research pass (search results synthesized this from aggregated web-search summaries rather than a directly fetched primary source); this claim should be treated as lower-confidence and re-verified against a primary source before being cited with high confidence.
- No source was found benchmarking codec-specific (e.g., H.264 vs H.265, specific bitrate/GOP settings typical of surveillance/drone video streams) compression augmentation specifically, as opposed to generic JPEG quality-factor augmentation — JPEG is the dominant compression-augmentation proxy used across all sources found, which may not fully represent video-codec compression artifacts (blocking, motion-compensation artifacts) relevant to a video-crop use case.
