# Domain Gap Mitigation for Frozen-Encoder Embedding Matching (Sharp Reference vs. Degraded/Distant Query)

## KQ1: Is "degrading the reference to simulate the query's capture conditions" (aerial_sim: downscale+blur before embedding) a documented, validated technique? Does it reliably help or can it backfire?

### Takeaway
Degrading a high-quality gallery/reference image to reduce the resolution/blur gap with a low-quality probe is a documented technique in the cross-resolution face recognition literature (framed as "downsample the gallery to match the probe" or "meet in the middle"), but almost all of that literature studies it as part of a *training/fine-tuning* pipeline (paired HR/LR data, matched-degradation training), not as a pure inference-time trick on a frozen encoder. Within that literature, naive/generic degradation (plain downsampling) is explicitly reported to under-deliver because it does not match real-world degradation statistics — a documented "backfire" mode — motivating calibrated degradation (estimating the blur/noise parameters from real target-domain data) over a fixed, hand-picked degradation level.

### Cited Findings
- In cross-resolution/low-resolution face recognition (LRFR), one of three documented strategies to bridge the HR-gallery vs LR-probe gap is to "down-sample high-resolution galleries to compare them with probes in low-resolution space" (the other two being probe super-resolution, and projecting both into a shared/unified feature space) — [Towards Understanding Cross Resolution Feature Matching for Surveillance Face Recognition, ACM Multimedia 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3503161.3548402).
- The "Meet-in-the-Middle" method explicitly degrades gallery samples by downsampling and applying an *estimated* Gaussian blur, where the blur parameter is calibrated via KL-divergence between the distribution of downsampled-gallery and upsampled-probe images (i.e., the degradation strength is fit to the actual target domain rather than fixed a priori) — [Meet-in-the-middle: Multi-scale upsampling and matching for cross-resolution face recognition, arXiv:2211.15225](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.15225).
- In low-resolution face recognition, "a straightforward solution ... is to use both high-resolution and corresponding down-sampled low-resolution images during training. However ... there is a significant difference between down-sampled LR images and LR images from surveillance cameras, leading to performance saturation" — i.e. plain/generic degradation of reference data does not fully close the gap and can leave a residual, unaddressed domain mismatch — [ScienceDirect, "Degradation model and attention guided distillation approach for low resolution face recognition"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417423033845), corroborated by a second source noting practitioners "hand-design degradation models" (combining blur, noise, and compression artifacts) precisely because Gaussian-blur-then-downsample alone diverges from real surveillance degradation — [Recognizability/related LRFR discussion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417423033845).
- Generic test-time augmentation is explicitly flagged as not universally beneficial: the authors of a mean-embedding test-time-augmentation ensembling paper state "the conventional data augmentation will not, most likely, fit for any problem," implying augmentation/degradation choices need to be tailored to the deployment domain rather than applied generically — [Mean Embeddings with Test-Time Data Augmentation for Ensembling of Representations, arXiv:2106.08038](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08038).
- In a CLIP-based person re-identification cross-dataset study, Random Erasing Augmentation (REA) *improved* within-domain performance but *harmed* cross-dataset (domain-gap) performance — direct evidence that an augmentation intended to increase robustness can backfire specifically under domain shift, a structurally similar risk to reference degradation reducing rather than improving cross-domain discriminability — [An Investigation of the Domain Gap in CLIP-Based Person Re-Identification, PMC11769178](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11769178/).

### Inferences
- The project's `aerial_sim` (downscale+blur reference before embedding with a *frozen* encoder, no retraining) is a plausible, literature-consistent instantiation of the "downsample gallery to probe's regime" strategy, but it is a novel application in the sense that essentially all cited precedent for gallery-degradation is embedded inside a training pipeline (paired-data fine-tuning or knowledge distillation), not a pure frozen-encoder pre-processing step. This is a meaningful gap between what's documented and what the project does — the *general principle* (reduce resolution mismatch by degrading the higher-quality image) is validated, but the *specific frozen-encoder-only, no-fine-tuning* execution has less direct precedent.
- The documented failure mode ("down-sampled LR ≠ real surveillance LR") maps directly onto the risk in the objective's own framing: a fixed, uncalibrated blur/downscale level is likely to either under-degrade (leaving residual domain gap) or over-degrade (destroying discriminative detail unnecessarily) relative to the real query video's actual degradation statistics. This argues for calibrating the aerial_sim degradation parameters against the real drone footage (analogous to Meet-in-the-Middle's KL-divergence blur fitting) rather than using an arbitrary fixed downscale/blur factor.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that studies gallery/reference degradation as a *pure inference-time* operation on a frozen ViT/DINO/CLIP encoder with no paired training data — the specific "frozen-encoder-only" version of this technique lacks direct literature validation either way (neither confirmed to reliably help nor confirmed to backfire in that exact regime).
- No controlled ablation was found that isolates "does over-degrading the reference make the embedding measurably *less discriminative* against other classes/distractors" (as opposed to merely more query-like) — this specific risk mentioned in the assignment is plausible by extrapolation from the augmentation-can-backfire evidence above, but not directly measured in any source found.

## KQ2: Is "shifting the prototype toward a domain-mean estimated from unlabeled target-domain data" (domain_calibration) well-established? Under what conditions does it help vs. hurt?

### Takeaway
Prototype/feature mean-shift calibration using unlabeled target-set statistics is a well-established family of techniques in transductive few-shot learning (prototype rectification, TIM, LaplacianShot) and in test-time normalization adaptation (transductive/test-time batch norm), and the literature consistently shows two failure conditions relevant to the project's concern: (1) naively swapping in target statistics without moderation can push features into an "unintended feature space" and hurt accuracy, which is why newer methods interpolate/weight the shift rather than applying it in full; and (2) unlabeled target samples that contain foreground/class-relevant content (not just domain/background style) can bias the prototype — a contamination risk explicitly studied under "shortcut learning of background."

### Cited Findings
- Prototype Rectification computes a shift term ξ = mean(support features) − mean(query features), used to correct for the distributional gap ("cross-class bias") between the support set and the (unlabeled) query set, and reports this materially improves few-shot accuracy, with larger gains in 1-shot (+8–9%) than 5-shot (+2–3%) settings — i.e. the benefit shrinks as the reference/support estimate becomes more reliable on its own — [Prototype Rectification for Few-Shot Learning, arXiv:1911.10713](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.10713).
- The same paper provides no explicit analysis of whether the shift can wash out discriminative class boundaries, though its own robustness table shows accuracy drops as unlabeled-set noise increases — evidence that the technique degrades gracefully but non-trivially in the presence of noisy/contaminated unlabeled data — [Prototype Rectification for Few-Shot Learning, arXiv:1911.10713](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.10713).
- LaplacianShot performs transductive inference with a Laplacian regularization term that encourages consistent labels among neighboring *query* samples (rather than a raw mean shift), and is reported to consistently outperform prior state-of-the-art across models/settings — an alternative, more conservative way to exploit unlabeled target-domain structure than a direct mean shift — [Laplacian regularized few-shot learning, ICML 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3524938.3526019).
- Transductive Information Maximization (TIM) maximizes mutual information between query images and predicted labels jointly with support-set cross-entropy, another established transductive approach using only unlabeled query statistics — [Transductive Information Maximization For Few-Shot Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Transductive-Information-Maximization-For-Few-Shot-Boudiaf-Ziko/1252cf714b3a4d6977496269248b2a91955aa447).
- "Rectifying the Shortcut Learning of Background for Few-Shot Learning" (COSOC) shows that background is a *shortcut* signal — helpful for in-distribution classification but "ungeneralizable beyond training categories" and harmful when adapting to new classes; the proposed fix extracts foreground objects before computing prototypes, based on the observation that foreground objects across images of the same class are more similar to each other than their backgrounds are — [arXiv:2107.07746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07746), NeurIPS 2021.
- On the normalization-statistics side: "Transductive Batch Normalization" (using the current test batch's mean/variance instead of source running stats) is reported to mitigate domain-shift degradation, but depends on "impractical assumptions that a test batch should be large enough and drawn from an i.i.d. stream," and shows "critical performance drop" when those assumptions are violated; more generally, "standardizing target feature activations using source statistics... can transform activations into an unintended feature space, resulting in misclassification" when the shift is applied without care — [TTN: A Domain-Shift Aware Batch Normalization in Test-Time Adaptation, arXiv:2302.05155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05155).
- TTN's fix is to *interpolate* between source (pretrained) statistics and target (test-time) statistics using a learnable interpolation weight, rather than fully replacing source statistics with target ones — direct evidence that the field treats "how strong should the target-domain shift be" as a first-class tunable parameter, not an all-or-nothing choice — [TTN, arXiv:2302.05155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05155).

### Inferences
- The project's `domain_calibration` (shift prototype toward the mean embedding of a few raw video frames) is directly analogous to the "cross-class bias" correction in Prototype Rectification and to feature-mean-shift-based TTA, both of which are established and shown to work — but both bodies of literature converge on the same caution: an *unmoderated, full-strength* shift risks the "unintended feature space" failure mode from the TTN paper. This supports using a bounded/interpolated shift strength (a "calibration weight" hyperparameter, analogous to TTN's learnable interpolation) rather than fully replacing the prototype's position with the domain mean.
- The COSOC / shortcut-learning-of-background finding is directly relevant to the project's stated contamination risk: if the sampled video frames used to compute the "domain mean" contain the target object itself (not just background/lighting/compression style), the resulting shift will conflate *domain style* with *object identity/appearance*, biasing the prototype toward whatever objects happen to be in the sampled frames rather than toward a pure exposure/compression correction. The literature's foreground/background distinction suggests the project should explicitly try to sample frames (or regions) that are unlikely to contain the target, or otherwise separate "style statistics" from "content statistics," to avoid this contamination — this is an inference extending COSOC's foreground/background argument to a video-frame-sampling context, not a directly tested claim in that paper.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that studies prototype/domain-mean shifting in the exact "single-class, few-reference-image, frozen-ViT, unlabeled-video-frame-domain-mean" setting the project uses (most transductive few-shot literature assumes N-way classification with many query samples per episode, not a single open-set target class against an unlabeled stream). Quantitative thresholds for "how strong a shift is too strong" were not found — TTN treats the interpolation weight as *learned*, not given as a fixed recommended value, so no directly transferable number exists for a non-trainable frozen pipeline.
- No source explicitly modeled or quantified "contamination when target-domain samples contain the object itself vs. only background" for a *mean-shift* prototype method specifically (COSOC addresses a related but distinct mechanism — shortcut learning during representation training/matching, not post-hoc prototype-mean contamination in a frozen-encoder pipeline).

## KQ3: What does face recognition / re-ID literature say about high-quality enrollment vs. low-quality probe matching, and what frozen-encoder-compatible techniques exist?

### Takeaway
This exact asymmetry is a well-studied, named problem — "low-resolution face recognition" (LRFR) / "cross-resolution face recognition" / "surveillance face recognition" — with three canonical solution families: (1) super-resolve the probe, (2) degrade/downsample the gallery to match the probe, (3) project both into a shared/unified feature space. Of these, only (1) and (2) are usable as pure pixel-space preprocessing in front of a frozen encoder without retraining; the "unified feature space" and most SOTA accuracy-maximizing methods (distillation, recognizability-index learning, identity-preserving networks) require training a dedicated model and are therefore NOT directly frozen-compatible.

### Cited Findings
- A 2024 review frames LRFR methods into two broad categories: "super-resolution for LR FR" and "resolution-robust feature representation for LR FR," confirming these are the two dominant paradigms in the field — [Low-resolution face recognition: Review, challenges and research directions, Computers and Electrical Engineering, 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.compeleceng.2024.109846).
- "A large body of work targets recognition when probes are degraded, most commonly by exposing the recognizer to degraded data during training so that high- and low-resolution embeddings become comparable" — confirming that the dominant approach in this literature is training-time, not a frozen/inference-only trick — [search synthesis over LRFR review sources](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.compeleceng.2024.109846).
- Recognizability Embedding Enhancement (CVPR 2023) improves very-low-resolution face recognition (VLRFR) by learning a "recognizability index" in embedding space and an index-diversion loss plus a perceptibility-aware attention mechanism, outperforming SOTA on TinyFace, SurvFace, and SCFace (HR-gallery vs. VLR-probe) benchmarks — but this requires training new loss functions/attention modules, i.e. is NOT frozen-encoder-compatible as-is — [arXiv:2304.10066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10066), CVPR 2023.
- "Meet-in-the-Middle" is explicitly framed around symmetric bridging: multi-scale upsampling (of the probe) combined with calibrated degradation (of the gallery) meeting at an intermediate resolution — supporting the idea that resolution-matching in *both directions* (not only degrading the reference) is a documented strategy, though the exact frozen vs. fine-tuned nature of its backbone could not be confirmed from available excerpts — [arXiv:2211.15225](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.15225) (methodology details not fully verifiable; PDF text extraction failed, so treat with caution).
- Drone-specific face recognition datasets/benchmarks quantify the practical severity of this gap: the DroneFace dataset spans ground distances 2–17 m and altitudes 1.5–5 m, and general biometric guidance requires "at least 50 pixels between eye centers for template extraction; 75–90 pixels for accurate recognition," meaning altitude/distance alone can push a face below usable resolution before any blur/compression is even considered — [EfficientFaceV2S: A lightweight model and benchmarking approach for drone-captured face recognition, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417425004087); see also [Multi-Resolution Face Recognition with Drones, ACM 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3441233.3441237) and [Face Recognition on Drones, ACM 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2750675.2750679).
- Aerial-ground person re-ID (a close analog to aerial object matching) shows severe accuracy collapse from ground-trained models applied to aerial data — reported examples include a drop from 94.2%/89.1% (ground datasets) to 63.4% (aerial UAV-Human) for one method, and from 84.9%/73.5% to 42.1% (aerial PRAI-1581) for another, attributed to altitude-driven viewpoint, pose, and resolution shift — [search synthesis over aerial-ground person re-ID sources, including Person Recognition in Aerial Surveillance: A Decade Survey, arXiv:2511.17674](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17674) (note: exact source attribution for these specific numbers could not be fully isolated to one paper from the search snippet; treat as directionally reliable but verify original numbers if precision is required).

### Inferences
- Of the three canonical LRFR strategies, gallery-degradation and probe-super-resolution are the two that are structurally compatible with the project's frozen-encoder constraint, since both operate purely on pixels *before* the frozen embedding call. The "unified feature space" and recognizability/distillation-based methods that dominate the highest-accuracy results in this literature are explicitly NOT usable without training, which should be flagged clearly to the project: the field's best-performing techniques are mostly off-limits under the frozen-backbone constraint, and the project's aerial_sim + domain_calibration combination sits in the more modest, training-free subset of the literature's toolbox.

### Gaps
- Full methodological detail of "Meet-in-the-Middle" (whether it fine-tunes the matching network or operates on frozen features) could not be extracted — PDF text extraction failed for the primary source and only search-snippet-level detail was obtainable. This should be treated as a partially-confirmed finding.
- No literature was found specifically addressing enrollment-vs-surveillance matching for *generic objects* (as opposed to faces/pedestrians) with a frozen DINOv2/CLIP-style encoder — the closest available analogs (face LRFR, aerial-ground person re-ID) are both identity-recognition domains with domain-specific architectures/losses, so transferability of exact numbers to a generic-object drone-matching setting is an extrapolation, not a directly validated claim.

## KQ4: Which TTA/transductive techniques are compatible with a frozen ViT encoder + cosine-similarity matching (no trainable head)?

### Takeaway
Embedding-space-only transductive methods (prototype rectification, TIM, LaplacianShot) and pure inference-time embedding ensembling (mean-embedding TTA, multi-scale aggregation, k-reciprocal re-ranking) are frozen-compatible because they operate entirely on the already-extracted embeddings/pixels with no backpropagation into the encoder. In contrast, the most famous general-purpose TTA methods — TENT and SHOT — both require updating model parameters (TENT: normalization affine parameters via entropy minimization backprop; SHOT: the entire feature extractor backbone) and are therefore NOT directly compatible with a genuinely frozen backbone.

### Cited Findings
- TENT "optimizes the model for confidence as measured by the entropy of its predictions" by "estimating [normalization] statistics and optimizing channel-wise affine transformations... batch-by-batch," which requires gradient updates to model parameters and a classifier whose output entropy can be measured — both of which are absent in a pure cosine-similarity embedding-matching pipeline with no classifier head — [Tent: Fully Test-Time Adaptation by Entropy Minimization, arXiv:2006.10726](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=uXl3bZLkr3c).
- SHOT "freezes the classifier module (hypothesis) of the source model and learns the target-specific feature extraction module" — i.e., it fine-tunes the backbone while freezing the head, the *inverse* of the project's frozen-backbone constraint, making it structurally incompatible without modification — [Do We Really Need to Access the Source Data? Source Hypothesis Transfer for UDA, ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/liang20a/liang20a.pdf).
- TENT is reported to be cheaper and adapt "with much cheaper operations" than SHOT but "cannot reach SHOT's performance when scaled to much larger datasets" — a direct efficiency/accuracy tradeoff between the two, both of which nonetheless require parameter updates — [search synthesis, TENT/SHOT comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726).
- Prototype Rectification, LaplacianShot, and TIM (detailed in KQ2) all operate purely on extracted feature vectors — computing shifts, Laplacian-regularized label assignments, or mutual-information objectives over embeddings — with no need to update encoder weights, making this family the most directly transferable to a frozen-ViT + cosine-similarity setting — [arXiv:1911.10713](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.10713); [LaplacianShot, ICML 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3524938.3526019).
- Mean-Embedding Test-Time Augmentation (MeTTA) averages embeddings across N augmented views of the same image with no parameter updates, and improves top-1 accuracy over a single central-crop view (e.g., +2.1% for a SimCLRv2 ResNet50 at N=32, +1.9% for a supervised ResNet50) — a purely inference-time, frozen-compatible technique — [arXiv:2106.08038](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08038).
- k-reciprocal re-ranking (used as a frozen-compatible post-processing component even inside a partly fine-tuned pipeline for far-distance aerial-ground re-ID) refines retrieval purely from pairwise distances after embeddings are computed, requiring no gradient updates — [Scale-Aware Vision-Language Adaptation for Extreme Far-Distance Video Person Re-identification, arXiv:2604.04183](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04183).

### Inferences
- Given the frozen-backbone constraint, the project's `domain_calibration` (prototype mean-shift) already sits in the *correct* compatible family (embedding-space-only transductive calibration) rather than the TENT/SHOT family (which would require abandoning the frozen-backbone constraint). This is a point in favor of the project's existing design choice, though — per KQ2 — the shift strength/interpolation question remains open.
- TTN's "learnable interpolation between source and target normalization statistics" cannot be directly ported to the project's setting without a training loop, since the interpolation weight is learned; however, the *concept* (don't fully replace source-domain statistics with target-domain statistics; blend them with a tunable, non-learned strength coefficient chosen by validation/heuristic) is a frozen-compatible simplification worth considering for `domain_calibration`.

### Gaps
- No literature was found on a retrieval/metric-learning-specific analogue of TENT/SHOT that is explicitly designed to keep the backbone frozen and instead adapts only a similarity function or prototype (i.e., no paper titled/framed as "TENT for retrieval" or "SHOT for cosine-similarity matching" was found). The embedding-space transductive few-shot methods (KQ2 family) are the closest available substitute, but they were developed for classification accuracy in episodic benchmarks, not open-set single-target retrieval, so their direct transferability to the project's exact setting is inferred, not confirmed by a matching paper.
- Whether ViT's LayerNorm (as opposed to CNN BatchNorm) makes transductive/test-time normalization statistics swapping (TBN/TTN) meaningfully different or inapplicable was not directly addressed in any source found — this is a relevant open question for DINOv2/CLIP ViT backbones specifically, since LayerNorm statistics are computed per-token/per-sample rather than per-batch, which may reduce the applicability of batch-statistics-based TTA methods to ViTs generally.

## KQ5: Does resolution/scale-matching matter more or less than blur-matching for ViT embeddings specifically?

### Takeaway
No source directly ran a controlled, head-to-head ablation isolating "scale/resolution mismatch" from "blur mismatch" for ViT-family embeddings, so this is answered by inference from two separate bodies of evidence: (a) ViT literature shows that resolution/patch-count mismatch relative to a model's pretraining regime can "severely degrade" performance because it changes how many tokens represent an object, while (b) DINOv2-specific robustness studies show embeddings are comparatively stable under blur. Together this weakly suggests scale/resolution-matching may matter at least as much as, and plausibly more than, blur-matching for ViT-style encoders — but this is an inference, not a directly cited comparative finding.

### Cited Findings
- "Modifying the preset resolution of a model may severely degrade the performance in Vision Transformers," and "the discrepancy between preset resolutions and actual input resolutions can significantly degrade ViT's performance on different-resolution images" — because ViT's self-attention and patch-embedding structure are sensitive to how an image resolves into a fixed patch grid — [MSPE: Multi-Scale Patch Embedding Prompts Vision Transformers to Any Resolution, arXiv:2405.18240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18240), NeurIPS 2024.
- DINOv2 was reported to maintain 88.44% accuracy even at 80% blur intensity in one evaluation, with features from degraded images described as "highly similar" to clean-image features, and DINOv2 "exhibits remarkable robustness against practical transformations like ... blur, JPEG compression" — evidence that blur specifically is comparatively well-tolerated by this encoder family — [search synthesis over DINOv2 robustness sources, e.g. FastDINOv2, arXiv:2507.03779, and related DINOv2 feature-robustness papers](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03779v1).
- Cross-resolution face recognition and drone face-recognition literature (KQ3) treat resolution/pixel-count (e.g., "at least 50 pixels between eye centers") as the primary, hard constraint for recognizability, with blur/noise typically treated as an additional, secondary degradation layered on top of an already resolution-limited image — [EfficientFaceV2S, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417425004087).

### Inferences
- Because a ViT's patch embedding allocates a *fixed number of tokens* to an object based on its pixel footprint relative to patch size, shrinking an object's apparent size (without necessarily blurring it) changes the effective "resolution" of representation allocated to it in a structurally different way than blur does (blur degrades detail within existing tokens but does not reduce token count). This structural argument, combined with the MSPE finding that resolution mismatch "severely degrades" ViT performance and DINOv2's reported blur tolerance, supports treating scale/resolution-matching (the project's `crop_context_margin`/`scale_calibration` mechanism) as at least as important — plausibly more important — than blur-matching alone for ViT-family encoders specifically. This should be treated as a reasoned inference from adjacent evidence, not a directly measured result.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that explicitly ablates "resolution/scale-matching only" vs. "blur-matching only" vs. "both" for DINOv2/DINOv3/CLIP embedding cosine-similarity retrieval. This is a genuine literature gap; the project's own future evaluation (if it runs an ablation of scale_calibration vs. aerial_sim's blur component in isolation) would likely be a novel data point rather than confirming an existing published result.

## KQ6: Is multi-scale/multi-degradation ensembling of the reference (instead of one calibrated degradation level) a documented, more robust alternative?

### Takeaway
There is no paper found that documents this *exact* technique (ensembling multiple simulated-distance/blur versions of a few-shot enrollment reference), but there is solid supporting precedent from two adjacent, well-established practices: (1) test-time-augmentation embedding ensembling (averaging embeddings over multiple augmented views of an image) is a documented, validated technique that improves accuracy and stability over a single view; and (2) multi-scale feature aggregation is a long-standing, established practice in image retrieval specifically. The project's proposed idea is best framed as a reasonable, literature-consistent extrapolation combining these two established principles, not as a directly cited technique in its own right.

### Cited Findings
- Mean-Embedding Test-Time Augmentation (MeTTA): averaging representations across multiple augmented views (N up to 32) consistently outperforms a single central-crop embedding — e.g. 71.7%→73.8% (+2.1%) for a SimCLRv2 ResNet50, 76.6%→78.5% (+1.9%) for a supervised ResNet50 — and the authors argue this works because it makes representations "invariant to [the tested] transformations by design" and "counteracts prediction jittering observed across different augmentations of similar objects" — [arXiv:2106.08038](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08038). Note: the augmentations tested were standard resize/random-crop/flip, not blur or simulated-distance degradation specifically, so extrapolating this result to a multi-degradation-level ensembling scheme is an inference.
- "Aggregated multi-scale representations" — averaging representations of an image computed at multiple scales — is described as an established practice in image retrieval, tracing to multi-scale cropping approaches (Gong et al. 2014) that "bring improvements for both image recognition and retrieval tasks" — [search synthesis on multi-crop/multi-scale augmentation in retrieval](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/multi-view-and-multi-crop-augmentation).
- "Learning Test-Time Augmentation for Content-Based Image Retrieval" specifically studies TTA for retrieval (rather than classification) and frames the goal as increasing "invariance of off-the-shelf features... without sacrificing their compactness and discriminability" via an ensemble of *learned* test-time augmentations — implying that a naive/unweighted single augmentation choice is suboptimal relative to a properly combined/learned ensemble, indirectly supporting the value of ensembling over picking one fixed degradation level — [arXiv:2002.01642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01642) (full methodology and quantitative results could not be extracted from available excerpts; title/abstract-level confirmation only).
- Query expansion (QE / αQE) — a related but distinct established retrieval technique — aggregates embeddings of multiple (retrieved, not degraded) images to form a more robust query representation, reflecting the same general principle that averaging over multiple related embeddings improves robustness in retrieval pipelines — [search synthesis on query expansion in image retrieval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01642).

### Inferences
- Combining the MeTTA finding (ensembling augmented embeddings beats a single view) with the multi-scale retrieval-aggregation precedent, and with the cross-resolution FR finding that a single fixed degradation level can mismatch real-world degradation statistics (KQ1), there is a reasonably strong indirect case that building several degraded reference embeddings at different simulated distances/blur levels and averaging (or max-pooling) them should be *more* robust than committing to one calibrated degradation level — because it hedges against miscalibration of that single level and provides some built-in invariance to the exact distance/blur the query video turns out to exhibit. This is presented as an evidence-based inference, not a directly cited result, since no source ensembles degradation *levels* of an enrollment image specifically.

### Gaps
- No direct precedent exists for ensembling degradation *levels* (as opposed to standard geometric/photometric augmentations) of a *reference/enrollment* image in a few-shot matching context. Quantitative comparison of "one well-calibrated degradation level" vs. "ensemble of several degradation levels" was not found anywhere and would need to be established empirically by the project itself.
- Whether max-pooling (vs. mean-pooling) across degraded-reference embeddings is preferable was not addressed in any source found — MeTTA and the multi-scale retrieval precedent both use averaging/mean-pooling, not max-pooling, so if the project considers max-pooling it would be diverging from the (limited) precedent that does exist.

## Cross-Cutting Note on the Frozen-Backbone Constraint

Several of the highest-performing techniques surfaced in this research (SHOT, matched-degradation fine-tuning/distillation for LRFR, recognizability-index learning, identity-preserving cross-resolution networks, and the backbone-scaling/selective-fine-tuning approach in the far-distance aerial-ground re-ID paper) all require training or fine-tuning and are therefore **not directly applicable** under the project's frozen-backbone constraint. The techniques that ARE directly compatible without any training are: (1) pixel-space preprocessing before embedding — reference degradation (aerial_sim) and probe super-resolution; (2) embedding-space-only transductive calibration — prototype/domain mean-shift (domain_calibration), Prototype Rectification-style shifting, LaplacianShot/TIM-style transductive inference; (3) pure inference-time embedding ensembling — mean/multi-scale TTA embedding pooling, k-reciprocal re-ranking, query expansion. The project's existing `aerial_sim` + `domain_calibration` design already sits within this frozen-compatible subset of the literature.
