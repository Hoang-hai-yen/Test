# Remote-Sensing / Aerial Foundation Model Encoders vs. DINOv2/DINOv3 for Few-Shot Re-ID Against Natural Clutter

Scope: SatMAE, Scale-MAE, RemoteCLIP, GeoCLIP, Prithvi (IBM/NASA), SkySense, RS5M/GeoRSCLIP, CROMA, SatlasPretrain, AnySat, SatDINO, and DINOv3-SAT493M, evaluated as candidate embedding backbones for a cosine-similarity few-shot re-identification pipeline on low-altitude drone imagery with natural clutter (leaves, dirt, rocks).

## Training objective (SSL / supervised / CLIP-style) and whether it gives a signal for separating discrete objects from background texture/clutter

### Takeaway
Most public remote-sensing foundation models are masked-autoencoder (MAE) style reconstruction models, which learn scene-level pixel/texture statistics rather than an explicit discriminative push between a discrete object and its surrounding texture; DINO-style self-distillation (DINOv3's own method, and the RS-specific SatDINO) and CLIP-style contrastive/text-alignment objectives are the exceptions, and there is direct published evidence that a DINO-style objective outperforms MAE-style objectives on remote-sensing benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- SatMAE is a masked-autoencoder (MAE) model pretrained on multispectral Sentinel-2 / fMoW imagery, using temporal and multi-spectral positional encodings; the objective is masked-patch pixel reconstruction, not instance discrimination — [Scale-MAE paper (arXiv:2212.14532)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14532); [SatMAE ResearchGate entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401457472_SatMAE_Pre-Training_Transformers_for_Temporal_and_Multi-Spectral_Satellite_Imagery)
- Scale-MAE is also MAE-based (masked reconstruction) but adds ground-sample-distance (GSD)-aware positional encoding and a Laplacian-pyramid multi-scale decoder; it outperformed SatMAE and ConvMAE on SpaceNet v1/v2, INRIA Aerial Image, and GID-15 — [Scale-MAE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14532)
- Prithvi (IBM/NASA) is also a ViT MAE pretrained on NASA HLS (Harmonized Landsat/Sentinel-2) imagery with 3D patch/positional embeddings for spatiotemporal inputs — reconstruction objective, not discriminative — [Prithvi-EO-2.0 paper (arXiv:2412.02732)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02732v3); [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/prithvi2-geospatial)
- CROMA combines contrastive learning across radar/optical modalities with an MAE reconstruction branch ("hybrid contrastive + MAE," using "FLIP masking") — so it does add an explicit contrastive (discriminative) term on top of reconstruction — [A Genealogy of Foundation Models in Remote Sensing (arXiv:2504.17177)](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17177); [CROMA GitHub](https://github.com/antofuller/CROMA)
- SkySense uses "Multi-Granularity Contrastive Learning" and "Geo-Context Prototype Learning" (a distillation/contrastive-family model, not MAE), explicitly designed for region-aware, multi-granularity discrimination — [SkySense CVPR 2024 poster page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2024/poster/29863); [SkySense GitHub](https://github.com/Jack-bo1220/SkySense)
- RemoteCLIP and GeoRSCLIP (RS5M) are CLIP-style image-text contrastive models: the embedding is trained to align with a caption of the whole scene, not to isolate a small discrete object from clutter. RemoteCLIP explicitly converts detection/segmentation box and mask annotations into captions (Box-to-Caption/Mask-to-Box) to scale its training data — [RemoteCLIP paper (arXiv:2306.11029)](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11029v4); [RemoteCLIP GitHub](https://github.com/ChenDelong1999/RemoteCLIP)
- GeoRSCLIP (fine-tuned CLIP on the RS5M 5M-pair image-text dataset) improved 3–20% on zero-shot classification, 3–6% on cross-modal retrieval, and 4–5% on semantic localization versus baseline CLIP — scene-classification/retrieval gains, not object-vs-clutter discrimination specifically — [RS5M/GeoRSCLIP GitHub](https://github.com/om-ai-lab/RS5M)
- SatDINO applies DINO-style self-distillation (not MAE) to satellite imagery, adding GSD encoding and adaptive view sampling; the authors report it "outperforms other state-of-the-art methods based on much more common masked autoencoders (MAE)" and achieves competitive results across multiple benchmarks. Code and trained models are stated to be available — [SatDINO (arXiv:2508.21402)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21402)
- DINOv3 itself (the generalist model already used as this project's baseline) uses self-distillation with multi-crop / Gram-anchoring, a method explicitly built for producing discriminative, retrieval-ready embeddings (dense + global features) — [DINOv3 paper (arXiv:2508.10104)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10104)

### Inferences
- Because MAE reconstructs raw pixels from the *whole* masked scene (including background), it optimizes for accurate texture/context statistics of leaves, dirt, and rock just as much as of the target object — there is no term in the loss that rewards separating a discrete object's identity from its surrounding clutter. This makes MAE-style RS encoders (SatMAE, Scale-MAE, Prithvi) a weaker theoretical fit for a cosine-similarity re-ID task than contrastive/self-distillation objectives.
- CLIP-style RS models (RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP) are trained against scene-level captions, so their global embedding is more likely to encode "what kind of scene/place is this" than "what specific small object is present" — this is a plausible mismatch for small-object re-identification against clutter, though it is an inference, not something these papers benchmark directly.
- SatDINO and CROMA's contrastive component are the two RS-specific approaches most philosophically aligned with the target use case (discriminative, not purely reconstructive), and are the most promising RS-specific candidates worth prototyping if DINOv3 alone proves insufficient.

### Gaps
- No paper found that explicitly benchmarks any RS foundation model's embedding on "does this crop contain the same discrete object as that crop, despite different clutter backgrounds" — a task-specific evaluation for small-object re-ID against natural background clutter appears absent from the RS foundation-model literature to date (2026).
- SatDINO's exact pretraining dataset/domain (satellite GSD, resolution) and parameter count were not retrievable from the abstract fetch and would need the full paper or GitHub README to confirm feasibility as a drop-in encoder.

## Training domain (satellite vs. low-altitude drone/aerial vs. ground-level) and relevance of that domain gap to a low-altitude drone use case with natural clutter

### Takeaway
Nearly every RS foundation model — including DINOv3-SAT493M — is trained on satellite or high-altitude sensor imagery at ground-sample-distances of roughly 0.1–30 m/pixel, a domain that is texturally very different from close-range, sub-centimeter-to-cm-GSD drone imagery of leaves/dirt/rocks; RemoteCLIP is the one model that deliberately includes drone/UAV imagery in training for resolution-agnostic representations, and GeoCLIP is trained on ground-level Flickr photos (not aerial) so it is domain-mismatched for this use case despite being framed as a "geolocation" model.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv3-SAT493M (facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m) was pretrained on "SAT-493M... 493 million 512×512 images sampled randomly from Maxar RGB ortho-rectified imagery at 0.6 meter resolution" — satellite-altitude GSD, not drone-altitude — [DINOv3-SAT493M model card (Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m)
- Prithvi-EO-2.0 was pretrained on NASA's Harmonized Landsat/Sentinel-2 (HLS) product at "30m granularity" using 4.2M samples across 6 spectral bands (Blue, Green, Red, Narrow NIR, SWIR, SWIR2) — far coarser than drone imagery — [Prithvi-EO-2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02732v3); it is benchmarked across tasks from "0.1m to 15m" resolution and reportedly beats 6 other geospatial foundation models across that resolution range — [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/prithvi2-geospatial)
- SatMAE trains on Sentinel-2 (10-60m/pixel bands) and fMoW (sub-meter to several-meter satellite imagery); Scale-MAE trains on fMoW-RGB — both satellite platforms — [Scale-MAE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14532)
- CROMA and SkySense train on Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2 (10-60m) plus, for SkySense, WorldView-3/4 (sub-meter commercial satellite) — still satellite platforms, no drone-altitude data — [Genealogy of Foundation Models in Remote Sensing](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17177); [SkySense poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2024/poster/29863)
- RemoteCLIP deliberately trains on "images taken from Sentinel-2, drones, and other aerial platforms" specifically to build a "resolution-agnostic" representation spanning satellite-to-drone GSDs, and incorporates UAV imagery to enlarge its pretraining set 12x — [RemoteCLIP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11029v4); the Genealogy survey notes RemoteCLIP nonetheless shows "reported underperformance in zero-shot image classification tasks," suggesting the multi-domain training does not automatically translate into best-in-class representations — [Genealogy survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17177)
- GeoCLIP is a CLIP-style model aligning locations with **ground-level Flickr photographs** for worldwide geolocalization — not aerial/satellite imagery at all — [GeoCLIP / rshf README](https://github.com/mvrl/rshf/blob/main/rshf/geoclip/README.md)
- SatlasPretrain's dataset does include "aerial imagery" alongside Sentinel-1/2 and Landsat-8/9, with 302M labels covering more of Earth than prior datasets — mixed satellite+aerial, still not drone/UAV-altitude close-range imagery — [SatlasPretrain blog (Ai2)](https://allenai.org/blog/satlaspretrain-models-foundation-models-for-satellite-and-aerial-imagery-1679ebe4bbfb)
- AnySat trains on GeoPlex, a mix of five datasets (PASTIS-HD, TreeSatAI-TS, PLANTED, FLAIR, S2NAIP) spanning "0.2-250m resolution" and 11 sensors including radar and optical — the finest end (0.2m) is closer to low-altitude but still framed as an "Earth Observation" (overhead/nadir) model, not ground-level or oblique drone photography — [AnySat paper (arXiv:2412.14123)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14123v1)
- Separately, DINOv2 (the generalist baseline) has already been used successfully for aerial/drone-native tasks: "DINOv2-small chosen for its self-supervised pretraining on diverse imagery yielding robust features that transfer to aerial animal crops" and "SHALA is an end-to-end framework built on a frozen DINOv2 backbone for vehicle re-identification" addressing cross-platform/cross-modal (aerial-ground) challenges — [SHALA paper (MDPI Remote Sensing)](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/18/3219)

### Inferences
- The domain gap is likely material: essentially every RS-specific encoder (SatMAE, Scale-MAE, Prithvi, CROMA, SkySense, SatlasPretrain, even DINOv3-SAT493M) is trained on overhead imagery with GSD in the tens-of-cm-to-tens-of-meters range, optimized for scene/land-cover context (fields, buildings, roads) rather than close-up textures of individual leaves, loose dirt, or rock surfaces that dominate a low-altitude drone crop. This is the opposite domain of what a small target's local texture/clutter separation problem needs.
- RemoteCLIP is the most domain-relevant RS-specific candidate purely on training-data composition (it explicitly includes UAV/drone imagery), but its CLIP objective (question 1) and the survey's note of "underperformance in zero-shot classification" both temper this.
- GeoCLIP should likely be excluded as a candidate outright — despite the "aerial/geo" branding implied by its name, it is trained on ground-level photographs for place recognition/geolocation, not for aerial object embedding.

### Gaps
- No source found that quantifies how much the satellite-vs-drone domain gap specifically hurts (or doesn't hurt) small-object-vs-clutter cosine-similarity performance; this would need direct empirical testing on the project's own drone crops.
- No RS foundation model found in this research that was pretrained primarily on low-altitude (<50m AGL) drone imagery of natural/unstructured terrain (forest floor, grass, dirt, rock) — this exact domain appears to be a gap in the current RS foundation-model landscape as of September 2026.

## Public availability of pretrained weights (HuggingFace/GitHub), license, and gating

### Takeaway
SatMAE, Scale-MAE, RemoteCLIP, GeoCLIP, Prithvi, RS5M/GeoRSCLIP, CROMA, SatlasPretrain, AnySat, and DINOv3-SAT493M all have some form of publicly downloadable weights; SkySense is the notable exception — its GitHub repo states an intent to release weights but, per available search results, has not done so.

### Cited Findings
- RemoteCLIP: pretrained checkpoints (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14) are on Hugging Face and downloadable via `huggingface_hub`; code is Apache-2.0 licensed — [RemoteCLIP GitHub](https://github.com/ChenDelong1999/RemoteCLIP); [RemoteCLIP paper page (HF)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2306.11029)
- Prithvi-EO-2.0 (300M and 600M, including a "-TL" temporal variant) is openly hosted on Hugging Face under `ibm-nasa-geospatial` and via IBM TerraTorch — [Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M-TL (HF)](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M-TL); [NASA-IMPACT/Prithvi-EO-2.0 GitHub](https://github.com/NASA-IMPACT/Prithvi-EO-2.0)
- CROMA: `CROMA_base.pt` and `CROMA_large.pt` weights are downloadable from the official GitHub repo — [CROMA GitHub](https://github.com/antofuller/CROMA)
- SatlasPretrain: model **weights** are released under **ODC-BY**; the code package (`satlaspretrain_models`) is Apache-2.0 licensed — [SatlasPretrain HF README](https://huggingface.co/allenai/satlas-pretrain); [allenai/satlaspretrain_models GitHub](https://github.com/allenai/satlaspretrain_models)
- RS5M / GeoRSCLIP: dataset and model code publicly released on GitHub — [RS5M GitHub](https://github.com/om-ai-lab/RS5M)
- AnySat: paper and code published at CVPR 2025 (arXiv:2412.14123); framework is presented as open (self-supervised training code across GeoPlex datasets) — [AnySat paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14123v1)
- DINOv3-SAT493M: two checkpoints exist — a ViT-7B/16 trained from scratch (6.7B params) and a ViT-L distilled from it; both hosted on Hugging Face (`facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m`, and via `timm`) under Meta's "DINOv3 License" (a custom/gated license, not a fully permissive open-source license — verify commercial-use terms before adoption) — [DINOv3-SAT493M model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m); [timm ViT-7B sat493m](https://huggingface.co/timm/vit_7b_patch16_dinov3.sat493m); note also a GitHub issue requesting access to the underlying SAT-493M *dataset* itself (the training data, not the weights, appears gated/restricted) — [dinov3 GitHub issue #132](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/issues/132)
- SkySense: the official GitHub repository states the authors "will release the pre-trained weights to facilitate future research," indicating that — as of the available search results — weights had not yet been publicly released despite the code repo existing since CVPR 2024 — [SkySense GitHub](https://github.com/Jack-bo1220/SkySense). A related, separately-published follow-on, SkySense++ (Nature Machine Intelligence 2025) has its own repo — [SkySensePlusPlus GitHub](https://github.com/kang-wu/SkySensePlusPlus) — but weight-release status for that repo was not verified in this research pass.
- SatDINO: paper states "Code and trained models are available" via GitHub, though the exact license was not confirmed in this pass — [SatDINO (arXiv:2508.21402)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21402)

### Inferences
- For a project already depending on off-the-shelf, no-gate weights (DINOv2/DINOv3 from `timm`/HF), RemoteCLIP, Prithvi, CROMA, SatlasPretrain, and RS5M/GeoRSCLIP are all realistic drop-in candidates on availability grounds alone.
- DINOv3-SAT493M is technically available but under a **restrictive custom license** distinct from a standard permissive license — this should be flagged to whoever handles licensing/compliance before use, especially since it is already a baseline in this pipeline per the git status (DINOv2/DINOv3 already in use).
- SkySense should be deprioritized/excluded as a near-term candidate purely because its weights are not confirmed publicly available, despite strong reported benchmark numbers.

### Gaps
- SatMAE's and Scale-MAE's exact current license terms were not directly confirmed (only that they are widely used as public baselines in follow-on papers, implying weights are accessible); a report writer should verify licensing directly against the official SatMAE (`sustainlab-group/SatMAE`) and Scale-MAE GitHub repos before recommending them for production/commercial use.
- GeoCLIP's license/gating status was not directly confirmed in this pass (available via the `rshf` package per search results, but exact license unverified).

## Model size / compute cost / inference latency relative to DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (86M params)

### Takeaway
RS foundation model sizes span a very wide range — from AnySat's ~100M (comparable to DINOv2 ViT-B) up to SkySense's ~2.09B and DINOv3-SAT493M's 6.7B-parameter ViT-7B — so compute cost is highly model-dependent, and several of the strongest-performing options (SkySense, DINOv3 ViT-7B) are an order of magnitude or more heavier than the current DINOv2 ViT-B/14 baseline.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv2 ViT-B/14 baseline: 86M parameters (given in the task; not independently re-verified here, but consistent with the well-known DINOv2 model card sizes).
- AnySat: "More than 75% of AnySat's 100M parameters are shared across all supported modalities, scales, and resolutions" — i.e., roughly 100M total, in the same order of magnitude as DINOv2 ViT-B — [AnySat paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14123v1)
- Prithvi-EO-2.0 is released in 300M and 600M parameter variants (plus a "-TL" temporal-localization variant), i.e., 3.5x–7x the size of DINOv2 ViT-B/14 — [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/prithvi2-geospatial); [Prithvi-EO-2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02732v3)
- SkySense is described as "billion-scale," with the Genealogy survey specifying "2.09B parameters" — roughly 24x the size of DINOv2 ViT-B/14 — [Genealogy of Foundation Models in Remote Sensing](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17177)
- DINOv3-SAT493M ships as a ViT-7B/16 (6,716M / 6.7B parameters, patch16, embed dim 4096, 32 heads, SwiGLU FFN, RoPE) trained from scratch, with only a single smaller distilled variant (a ViT-L) — no ViT-B-sized satellite-native checkpoint exists in this family — [DINOv3-SAT493M model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m). The ViT-7B is ~78x larger than DINOv2 ViT-B/14; even the distilled ViT-L (typically ~300M for DINO-family ViT-L) is still ~3.5x larger.
- RemoteCLIP ships in ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, and ViT-L/14 sizes — the ViT-B/32 variant is in the same broad size class as DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (both "ViT-Base"), making it the closest RS-specific match on compute cost — [RemoteCLIP GitHub](https://github.com/ChenDelong1999/RemoteCLIP)
- CROMA is released in "base" and "large" sizes (`CROMA_base.pt`, `CROMA_large.pt`) — base is plausibly comparable to DINOv2 ViT-B, though exact parameter counts were not confirmed in this pass — [CROMA GitHub](https://github.com/antofuller/CROMA)

### Inferences
- If inference latency/compute parity with the current DINOv2 ViT-B/14 baseline matters, RemoteCLIP ViT-B/32, CROMA-base, and AnySat (~100M) are the realistic same-class candidates; Prithvi (300-600M), the DINOv3-SAT493M ViT-L distillation, and especially SkySense (2.09B) and DINOv3 ViT-7B (6.7B) would substantially increase inference cost and are likely impractical for a latency-sensitive drone pipeline without significant infrastructure investment or further distillation.

### Gaps
- Exact parameter counts for SatMAE, Scale-MAE, SatlasPretrain, GeoCLIP, GeoRSCLIP, and SatDINO backbones were not confirmed in this research pass (their papers typically use standard ViT-Base/Large or Swin backbones, but specific configurations should be checked directly against each repo before a final sizing decision).
- No direct wall-clock inference latency benchmarks (e.g., ms/crop on a specific GPU) were found for any of these models relative to DINOv2 ViT-B/14; this would require local benchmarking.

## Published evidence of outperforming DINOv2/DINOv3 on foreground-vs-background discrimination, few-shot re-ID, open-set matching, or small-object retrieval (not generic classification/segmentation)

### Takeaway
The strongest and most directly relevant piece of evidence found is a controlled 2026 comparison paper concluding that **generalist vision foundation models (DINOv2/DINOv3) are competitive with, and in some cases outperform, existing electro-optical remote-sensing-specific models on retrieval tasks**, and that domain-specific pretraining alone does not guarantee better retrieval-oriented representations — this directly undercuts the case for switching away from DINOv2/DINOv3 toward an RS-specific encoder for this project's re-ID task.

### Cited Findings
- "Rethinking Electro-Optical Vision Foundation Models for Remote Sensing Retrieval: A Controlled Comparison with Generalist VFM" states in its abstract (verified direct quote): **"Strong generalist vision foundation models are competitive with, and in some cases outperform, existing EO-specific models."** It further finds that "EO-specific models show substantial degradation in cross-scene evaluation, while generalist models demonstrate more stable transfer capabilities," and concludes domain-specific pretraining alone does not guarantee superior retrieval-oriented representations, calling for future EO models to better incorporate "physical, spatial, spectral, and geographic characteristics of remote sensing imagery" — [arXiv:2605.02283](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02283) (abstract verified at [arxiv.org/abs/2605.02283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02283)). Models compared reportedly include SatMAE, Scale-MAE, RemoteCLIP, CROMA, and RVSA against DINOv2/DINOv3, per an initial automated extraction of the PDF body — **this specific model-list and task-list detail (retrieval, few-shot, foreground-background, small-object) could not be independently re-verified against the abstract alone and should be treated as lower-confidence than the abstract quote itself; a report writer should pull the full PDF directly to confirm the exact experimental setup before citing specifics beyond the verified abstract.**
- DINOv3-SAT493M (the satellite-pretrained DINOv3 variant) **underperforms** the standard web/natural-image-pretrained DINOv3 (LVD-1689M / "lvd1618m") on GEO-Bench: "the authors additionally pre-train a DINOv3 model on satellite imagery and find that it does not match the performance of the natural-imagery-trained version on GEO-Bench" — cited from the "DINO Soars" paper's discussion of the DINOv3 paper's own findings — [DINO Soars (arXiv:2605.03175)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03175). This is a directly relevant, citable data point: even Meta's own satellite-domain-adapted DINOv3 checkpoint does not beat the generalist DINOv3 on a remote-sensing benchmark suite, reinforcing that domain-specific pretraining is not automatically better for this model family.
- SatDINO (DINO-style, RS-specific) reports outperforming MAE-based RS models on multiple benchmarks, but the comparison in the retrieved abstract is against other RS-specific MAE methods, not against generalist DINOv2/DINOv3 — [arXiv:2508.21402](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21402)
- DINOv2 is independently reported as already effective for aerial/drone-native discriminative tasks outside the RS-foundation-model literature: used as a frozen backbone for vehicle re-identification under aerial-ground viewpoint gaps (SHALA) and for aerial animal-crop pose classification, citing its self-supervised pretraining on diverse imagery as yielding "robust features that transfer to aerial animal crops" — [SHALA (MDPI Remote Sensing, 2072-4292/18/18/3219)](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/18/3219)
- SkySense claims to "surpass 18 recent RSFMs [remote sensing foundation models] in all test scenarios" and to outperform GFM, SatlasPretrain, and Scale-MAE "by a large margin" — but these are classification/segmentation/detection benchmark claims from the SkySense authors themselves, not independently verified foreground-vs-background or re-ID evaluations, and (per the availability section above) its weights are not confirmed publicly downloadable for independent verification — [SkySense GitHub](https://github.com/Jack-bo1220/SkySense)
- A separate transfer-learning study, "How do Self-Supervised Remote Sensing Vision Models Transfer to Downstream Tasks?", evaluates six GeoFMs across classification/regression/segmentation and finds that **"model rankings change across tasks and adaptation settings"** — i.e., no single RS-specific SSL model is universally best — though its verified abstract does not mention DINOv2/DINOv3 by name, so it cannot be used to directly support or refute RS-specific-vs-generalist superiority — [arXiv:2606.13896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13896)

### Inferences
- Across the sources found, there is no confirmed, independently-verifiable published benchmark showing an RS-specific foundation model beating DINOv2/DINOv3 specifically on foreground-object-vs-background discrimination, few-shot re-identification, open-set matching, or small-object retrieval. The one paper whose title and abstract most directly address "retrieval" concludes generalist models are competitive-to-superior, and the satellite-adapted DINOv3 variant itself underperforms the generalist DINOv3 on GEO-Bench — both pieces of verified evidence point toward the project's existing DINOv2/DINOv3 choice being reasonable rather than a gap to fix with an RS-specific swap.
- Where RS-specific models do claim superiority (SkySense's "surpasses 18 RSFMs," GeoRSCLIP's zero-shot gains), the claims are on generic classification/segmentation/retrieval-of-scenes benchmarks — exactly the category the task explicitly asked to discount in favor of foreground/background and small-object evidence.

### Gaps
- The detailed experimental setup of arXiv:2605.02283 (exact task definitions for "foreground-background discrimination" and "small object detection/clutter handling," and the precise per-model score table) could not be confirmed beyond the abstract in this research pass — the full PDF should be re-fetched and read directly (not summarized by an automated tool) before quoting specific numbers or task claims beyond the verified abstract sentence above.
- No paper was found that directly benchmarks any of these encoders on a wildlife/small-target re-identification task set against natural ground clutter (forest floor, dirt, rock) at drone altitude — this exact evaluation appears to not exist yet in the public literature as of September 2026, meaning the project would likely need to run its own empirical comparison rather than rely on a published result.

## Usability as a drop-in global embedding extractor (single feature vector per crop, cosine similarity) without a full fine-tuning pipeline

### Takeaway
RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP, CROMA, Prithvi, SatlasPretrain, AnySat, and DINOv3-SAT493M all expose a standard encoder that can be loaded via HuggingFace/`timm`/official GitHub code and pooled to a global feature vector (CLS token, projection head output, or global-average-pooled patch tokens) for cosine-similarity use without fine-tuning, mirroring how DINOv2/DINOv3 are already used in this project; MAE-style models (SatMAE, Scale-MAE, Prithvi) require slightly more care because their pretraining task is reconstruction-oriented and their encoders were not trained with a pooled global-embedding objective the way CLIP/DINO models were.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv3-SAT493M's model card explicitly frames it as a drop-in backbone: it "function[s] as vision backbones providing multi-purpose features for downstream tasks," working "effectively as drop-in feature extractors without fine-tuning, suitable for image retrieval using nearest neighbors," returning "a class token, patch tokens (and register tokens)" — i.e., directly analogous to how DINOv2/DINOv3 web variants are already used in this pipeline — [DINOv3-SAT493M model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov3-vit7b16-pretrain-sat493m)
- RemoteCLIP and GeoRSCLIP are CLIP-style models, and CLIP's image encoder is designed from the ground up to output a single pooled global embedding for exactly this kind of use (zero-shot classification, retrieval, k-NN classification, few-shot classification are all listed as supported RemoteCLIP downstream uses) — [RemoteCLIP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11029v4)
- CROMA and Prithvi both publish loadable checkpoint files (`CROMA_base.pt`/`CROMA_large.pt`; HF `ibm-nasa-geospatial` checkpoints) intended for downstream fine-tuning/feature extraction via companion code (CROMA repo; IBM TerraTorch for Prithvi), implying encoder-only, no-fine-tune usage is supported, though both are primarily documented/benchmarked in the RS literature via task-specific fine-tuning (segmentation/classification heads) rather than pure frozen cosine-similarity retrieval — [CROMA GitHub](https://github.com/antofuller/CROMA); [NASA-IMPACT/Prithvi-EO-2.0 GitHub](https://github.com/NASA-IMPACT/Prithvi-EO-2.0)
- SatMAE/Scale-MAE/Prithvi are MAE encoders; the MAE encoder half (discarding the decoder) is the standard way these are reused as frozen feature extractors, but because they were optimized for pixel reconstruction rather than a pooled discriminative embedding, most published downstream use of them is via **fine-tuning** a task head, not frozen cosine-similarity matching — [Scale-MAE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14532)

### Inferences
- On pure "load off-the-shelf, no fine-tuning needed" usability, RemoteCLIP (CLIP-style) and DINOv3-SAT493M (DINO-style) are the best-matched to the project's current DINOv2/DINOv3-based cosine-similarity pipeline, since both were trained with an explicit global-embedding objective. MAE-based RS models (SatMAE, Scale-MAE, Prithvi) would likely need at minimum a pooling-strategy decision (CLS vs. mean-pooled patch tokens) and probably light contrastive fine-tuning/calibration before their embeddings are reliable for cosine-similarity few-shot re-ID, since that is not what their pretraining objective targets.

### Gaps
- No source found benchmarks the *quality* of frozen/pooled embeddings from SatMAE, Scale-MAE, or Prithvi specifically for nearest-neighbor/cosine-similarity retrieval (as opposed to fine-tuned linear-probe or segmentation-head performance) — this is a meaningful gap since it is the exact usage pattern the project needs, and it should be tested empirically if any MAE-based RS model is considered.
